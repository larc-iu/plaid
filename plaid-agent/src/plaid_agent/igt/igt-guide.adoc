= Introduction

**Plaid IGT** is an editor for interlinear glossed text (IGT), for documentary and field linguists.
It runs in a web browser and covers transcription, interlinear analysis, and the lexicon in one program.

Key features include:

* **Transcription.** Attach audio or video to a document and transcribe it one time-aligned segment at a time, with playback speed control and a speaker label on each segment. The transcript is the text that is then analyzed.
* **Interlinear analysis.** Divide the text into sentences, words, and morphemes, and annotate them with the fields your project defines: glosses, parts of speech, free translations, and others.
* **Vocabulary linking.** Words and morphemes may be linked to entries in a vocabulary. A linked entry supplies its gloss wherever the form occurs, so a word is analyzed consistently across the corpus. Homonyms, affixes and clitics, and multi-word expressions are supported.
* **Import and export.** Import a FieldWorks (FLEx) backup or interlinear texts, ELAN files, Praat TextGrids, or a CLDF dataset. Export to FLEx (`.flextext` with a LIFT lexicon), ELAN (`.eaf`), Praat (`.TextGrid`), CLDF, plain text, or a LaTeX book, or copy a sentence as LaTeX.
* **Collaboration.** A project is shared with its members, each of which may have reader, writer, or maintainer privileges. A central database lets all users see the latest state of the data, with no import/export required.
* **Audited edits.** Every change is recorded in an audit log with its author, and any document can be viewed as it was at an earlier time.
* **Pluggable AI assistance.** A connected AI model can transcribe a recording, draft translations, or propose segmentations and glosses. Machine output is marked until a person confirms it, and a service never overwrites human work.
* **Special character entry support.** Praat's backslash codes work in every field: `\sw` for ə, `\ng` for ŋ, `\?g` for ʔ. Additional codes may also be defined.
* **Comments.** Any sentence, word, morpheme, or annotation can carry a comment thread to allow in-context discussion of analyses.
* **Bulk edit and query tools.** Search every document by form or annotation, respell a word across the corpus, and hold a field to a controlled list of tags.

The chapters below follow the order of the work: a project and its documents, the recording, the analysis, the lexicon, and then the tools around them.
The last chapter, <<_reference>>, holds the exhaustive rules that the other chapters only summarize.

= Getting Started

Plaid IGT is served by your Plaid server. There is nothing to install.
Open the editor in your browser at the `/igt/` path on that server.
If you are running Plaid locally with the default settings, that is:

----
http://localhost:8080/igt/
----

Sign in with the email address and password your project administrator gave you, or with the password you chose when you opened an invitation link (see <<_sharing_and_permissions>>).
Your name at the top right opens a menu with **Profile** and **Sign out**.
On your profile you set a display name and a picture, change your password, and mint API tokens.

The editor has a few levels you move between:

* **Projects**: the list of all projects you can access. This is your home screen after signing in.
* **Documents**: the texts inside one project. Beside the document list sit the project's own tabs: **Search**, **Guidelines**, **Assistant** (when an assistant is running), **Jobs**, and **Export** for everyone, and **Bulk Edit**, **Validation**, **Activity**, and **Settings** for maintainers.
* **A document's tabs**: **Baseline**, **Media**, **Tokenize**, **Analyze**, **Comments**, **Export**, and **Details**, switched at the top of an open document.

The **Vocabularies** link in the header leads to the vocabularies (see <<_vocabularies>>), and **Guide** opens this document.

TIP: The Tokenize, Analyze, and Media tabs each have a **?** button that opens a legend of the gestures and keys that tab answers to. If you forget a shortcut, that is the quickest place to look.

= Projects

A **project** holds a collection of documents along with everything they share: the annotation fields, the orthographies, the tagsets, the vocabularies it draws on, and its members.
Create a project from the Projects screen, either blank (a short wizard asks for those settings) or from imported data (see <<_import_and_export>>).
Click a project to open its documents.

Because so much is configured per project, two projects can look quite different.
A project for a field methods course might have a single Gloss field and a free translation, while a documentation project adds a part of speech, a phonetic orthography, and a lexicon.

== Sentences, words, and morphemes

Interlinear glossed text is organized at three **scopes**, and the annotation fields and the Analyze grid are built around them:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Scope | What it is

| **Sentence**
| A stretch of the baseline text. Sentence fields hold things like the free translation, the speaker, and notes.

| **Word**
| A token within a sentence. Word fields hold things like a part of speech or a word-level gloss.

| **Morpheme**
| A piece of a word. The morpheme gloss and any other morpheme fields live here.
|===

A word is divided into one or more morphemes.
A simple word is a single morpheme, while a word like English _cats_ is two, _cat_ and _-s_.
Every field in a project belongs to exactly one scope, which decides which row of the grid it appears on.

= Documents

A **document** is one text of a project: a story, a conversation, an elicitation session.
It is built up on its tabs, from the raw text on **Baseline** through the words on **Tokenize** to the analysis on **Analyze**.
This chapter covers the document list and the tabs that hold the text.
The recording has a chapter of its own (see <<_media>>), and so does the analysis (see <<_analyze>>).

== Document list

A project's **Documents** tab lists its texts with their word counts, when each was last changed by anyone, and when you last changed it yourself.
Click a column heading to sort by it, and again to reverse it.
Sorting by **Your last edit** is the quickest way back to what you were working on.

**New document** offers two ways to start:

* **Blank document** asks for a name and opens the new, empty document on its Baseline tab.
* **Import from ELAN or Praat** brings `.eaf` and `.TextGrid` files into the project (see <<_importing>>).

A member who is not a maintainer is asked for the name straight away.

== Details

The **Details** tab holds the document's name and its document-level fields, such as speaker, date, and notes.
Which fields appear is configured per project (see <<_annotation>>).
A field can be held to a tagset, so that a genre is always one of a fixed set (see <<_tagsets>>).

This is also where a document is renamed, copied, and deleted.
**Copy** makes a second document in the same project, with the same text, annotations, vocabulary links, and recording, but no comments.
A recording the server cannot copy is left out, and the message after the copy says so.

**Text direction** says which way the document's words are laid out.
On **Automatic**, a document written in Arabic, Hebrew, or any other right-to-left script lays its words out right to left in the Analyze grid, with the row labels on the right.
Set it by hand for a document the text cannot speak for, such as a right-to-left language written in a Latin transliteration.
Each field still reads in its own direction, so an English gloss reads left to right under an Arabic word.

== Baseline

The **Baseline** tab holds the raw text you are analyzing, in whatever orthography you work in.
Paste or type it, one sentence per line, and save.
On the first save each line becomes a sentence.

Each save also splits the text it adds into words, unless the project has **Tokenize new text** off (see <<_annotation>>).
A script written without spaces between words, such as Chinese, Japanese, or Thai, is not split into words, so make its words on the Tokenize tab.

=== Later edits

You can edit the baseline later too, and the sentences, words, and annotations already there are kept.
The words follow your typing by a few fixed rules:

* A word you change keeps its analysis as long as one of its letters is left.
* A word whose text you delete is deleted with its annotations.
* New text becomes words of its own, and text typed at either end of a sentence joins that sentence.
* A space typed inside a word splits it in two. Deleting a space does not merge words, so do that on the Tokenize tab.
* A sentence whose exact text you replace keeps its translation and its other fields.

A word you select whole and type over keeps the letters the new word shares with it at either end.
So what happens to its analysis depends on how alike the two are:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Typed over _cat_ | Result

| _cut_
| The word is kept with the glosses of _cat_, since the two start and end alike.

| _cat_
| Nothing changes.

| _dog_
| The word is deleted with its annotations, and _dog_ is new text.
|===

A save that deletes words, segments, or sentences with something on them asks first, with counts.
The remaining rules, such as those for a paste over several words, are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_baseline>>.

=== Two people editing

When someone else saved the baseline while you were editing it, **Save changes** puts your changes onto their text.
If you both changed the same passage, the save is refused and your text stays in the box.
**Cancel** then shows the saved text, and you make the edit again.

=== Additional orthographies

A project may also have **additional orthographies**, such as IPA, another script, or a normalized form.
Each is a row of its own under the **Baseline** row in the Analyze grid, with its own value for each word.
Adding one does not fill it from the baseline: it starts empty, and is filled in by hand or by a tool.
They are set under **Settings → Text and vocab** (see <<_text_and_vocab>>).

== Tokenize

The **Tokenize** tab divides the baseline into sentences and words.
Words are highlighted, and text that is not yet part of any word is plain.
Nothing is annotated here. That happens on the Analyze tab.

To tokenize by hand:

* **Drag** across plain text to make a word of it, or across several words to merge them.
* **Click** a word to split it at a character. **Esc** closes the split points.
* **Right-click** a word to delete it.
* **Ctrl/Cmd+click** a word to split its sentence there, making that word the first of a new one.
* **Alt+click** a word to open it on the Analyze tab.
* The **↑** button on a sentence merges it with the previous one.

A change that would delete annotations asks first, and counts them.

To tokenize the whole document at once, use **Tokenize** in the **Tokens** header.
The built-in **Rule-based punctuation** splitter finds words and leaves sentence breaks as they are, and a tokenization service can split sentences too (see <<_services>>).
Words already there are kept, so text added later can be tokenized by running it again.

Three more buttons sit beside it:

* **Clear tokens** and **Reset sentences** start over.
* **Split at segments** adds a sentence break wherever a segment from the Media tab starts, so a recording cut into utterances becomes one sentence per utterance.

TIP: A word that came out split can be repaired by dragging across its pieces, which merges them into one word.

=== Ignored tokens

A project can mark certain tokens as **ignored**, usually punctuation.
Ignored tokens are not words.
They read as plain text everywhere, in the grid, in counts, searches and exports, with no annotation and no morphemes.

The built-in splitter breaks words at punctuation.
An orthography that writes a glottal stop or an ejective with an apostrophe therefore needs that character listed under **Characters that behave as letters** in **Settings → Annotation** (see <<_annotation>>).
Then `k'a` stays one word.

= Media

The **Media** tab attaches an audio or video recording to the document and holds its transcript as time-aligned **segments**.
Each segment is a stretch of the recording with its text, which becomes part of the baseline, and an optional speaker label.
Media is optional, and a document with none is simply a text.

== Recordings

Common audio and video formats are accepted (MP3, WAV, M4A, MP4, WebM, and others).
Until the whole file has gone up, **Cancel** stops the upload, or the conversion or mix before it, and nothing is stored.

A recording has to be one the browser can play.
A WAV that browsers cannot play, such as the ADPCM files ELAN and some recorders write, is converted to plain PCM when you choose it.
Any other file the browser cannot play is refused, as in `story.wav cannot be played in a browser (GSM 6.10 WAV).`
Convert such a file to MP3 or PCM WAV with an audio editor first.

A large recording can be sent as audio only.
Choosing one under **Single** offers to convert it to MP3 in the browser, mono at 16 kHz, about 14 MB an hour.
The timing is unchanged, but the picture is dropped and the sound is no longer good enough for phonetic measurement.

== Recordings with one file per speaker

The upload card has two modes:

* **Single** takes one file, and the recording plays as it was recorded, a stereo one in stereo.
* **Per speaker** is for a conversation recorded with a microphone per speaker, which gives several files that share one clock.

Under **Per speaker**, choose all the files, name the speaker of each, and **Mix recordings** makes one recording of them with a channel per file.
A file made elsewhere with a speaker on each channel can be chosen alone, and **Speakers per channel** then takes a speaker for each channel.

A recording uploaded this way shows a **channel list** under the player.
Each channel has its speaker, which you can change, and **Mute** and **Solo**, which only change what you hear.
With the speakers filled in, each channel works on its own:

* The timeline draws one waveform lane per channel in the speaker's colour.
* Speech detection and transcription list the channels, so each runs on one channel alone.
* Every proposal and segment they make carries that channel's speaker.

Two speakers' segments may overlap in time.

The mix is made once, when the files are uploaded.
To add a speaker later, delete the recording and upload all the files again.
The options of a mix, and what happens to each kind of file, are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_media>>.

== Transcript

The transcript is a list of segments in time order, and it is built for typing what you hear.
Play the recording, then type the utterance you just heard into the **New segment** row and press Enter.
The new segment runs from the end of the previous one to wherever playback is when you save.
It takes the speaker of the last segment made in this document since the page was loaded, so a run of turns by one speaker needs nothing more than Enter.

Every segment is a row you can edit:

* Moving into a row plays its segment. Turn off **Play segment on entry** to play by hand instead.
* **Enter** saves the row and moves to the next. **Tab** moves to the next field within a row.
* **↑** and **↓** move to the row above or below, from the start or end of the text.
* **Esc** puts a row back the way it was.
* **Shift+Space** pauses and resumes the segment you are in without leaving the row.
* **Shift+←** and **Shift+→** move playback one second.
* **Space** plays and pauses the whole recording when you are not typing in a box.
* **Alt+Enter** splits the segment at playback, its text between the words at the caret, and **Alt+M** merges it with the next segment.

Splitting and merging segments leaves the words, their annotations and the sentences unchanged, so two segments in different sentences do not merge.

A change to a row's text is saved as it is on the Baseline tab (see <<_later_edits>>).
The words in it keep their analysis unless all of a word's letters are replaced, and a segment whose exact text you replace keeps its times.
A change that deletes annotations this way asks first, with counts.

When someone else changed the same segment's text while you were editing it, the row shows their text with yours under it.
**Enter** keeps yours, and **Esc** lets it go.

=== Times, speed and looping

A segment's start and end times are boxes of digits in its row.
Type into the box under the caret, or step it with **↑** and **↓**: milliseconds by 10, or by 100 with Shift.
A segment can never run into its neighbours.

The speed slider runs from 0.25× to 5×, and the loop button repeats the segment you are on until you pause.

=== Deleting segments

The trash on a row deletes the segment.
When its text carries annotations, it asks first:

* **Delete text** deletes the text and its annotations along with the segment.
* **Keep text** leaves the text in the baseline.

**Delete segments**, in the **Transcript** header, removes segments in bulk: all of them, the ones with no text, or the ones whose text is a value you give.

=== Segments and sentences

Segments typed in the transcript go onto one line of the baseline.
**Split at segments** on the Tokenize tab makes each of them a sentence, and the transcript links to it.

=== Machine transcription

**Transcribe**, also in the **Transcript** header, has a transcription service write segments and their text into the document (see <<_services>>).
Its segments are marked as machine-made until you edit or confirm them.
Everything already in the document stays, so running it again adds only what is new.
To transcribe from scratch, clear the text on the Baseline tab first.

== Timeline

The timeline shows the same segments against the waveform.
You work on it with the mouse:

* Drag across an empty stretch to add a segment anywhere in the recording.
* Drag a segment's edge to trim it.
* Click a segment to hear it and edit its row.
* **Ctrl/Cmd+wheel** zooms the timeline, and the wheel alone pans it.
* Click the ruler above it, which shows the time, to move playback there.

The card over a selected segment has buttons to split it at playback and to merge it with the next one.

A dragged stretch takes either new text or text already in the baseline.
Under **Existing text**, select the words the segment covers.
This is how you align a text that was typed before the recording was attached.

The timeline colors each speaker's segments alike.
Two segments may overlap in time only when both have a speaker and the speakers differ, which is how cross-talk is recorded.
A dragged edge stops at any segment it may not overlap.

== Speech detection

The waveform button in the **Recording** header detects speech in the recording and proposes where the utterances are.
It runs in the browser and needs no service.

A proposal is a stretch of time with no text, dashed on the timeline and in the transcript.
Type into one and it becomes an ordinary segment with those times.
The **×** on its row discards it, and **Discard** in the detection dialog discards them all.

Proposals are kept with the document, so they can be transcribed another day or by someone else.
**Detect** replaces them.
Detection leaves alone any stretch that already has a segment, so you can run it again after using some of what it proposed.

The built-in detector's options change what is proposed:

* **Speech threshold**: how sure the model must be before a moment counts as speech. Raise it on a noisy recording.
* **Shortest silence**: a pause shorter than this does not end a segment.
* **Shortest segment**: a stretch of speech shorter than this is dropped.
* **Longest segment**: a longer stretch is split at the widest pause inside it.
* **Padding**: added to each end of a segment.

= Analyze

The **Analyze** tab is where interlinearization happens.
It shows each sentence with its words, the morphemes under each word, and a row for every annotation field.
Long documents are shown twenty-five sentences at a time, with a pager above and below the grid.

Click a row's label to choose which rows show.
A minimized row stays as a thin stripe you can click back open.

A document of more than 15,000 words is marked **Long document**, since editing slows down past that length.
Split it into shorter ones.

== Segmenting and glossing

Each word starts as a single morpheme spelled like the word.
Break it into morphemes in the morpheme row, and edit their forms:

* Type `-` in a morpheme to split it at the cursor.
* Type `=` to split at a clitic boundary (see <<_morpheme_types>>).
* **Backspace** at the start of a morpheme merges it into the previous one. The merged-in morpheme's gloss, link and comments are not kept.
* **Alt+0** types the zero morph ∅ (see <<_the_zero_morph>>).

Then fill the gloss and any other word or morpheme fields your project defines.
The free translation, the speaker, and other sentence fields are on each sentence's own row.

Move around the grid with **Enter** or **Tab** (next cell, Shift for the previous one), **↑** and **↓** (rows), and **←** and **→** from the ends of a value.
**Alt+click** a word to open it on the Tokenize tab.
The full set of gestures is collected under <<_keyboard_shortcuts>>.

== Guesses and value lists

The editor helps you reuse what the project already has, in three ways:

* **Guesses.** An empty cell may show a faint guess, such as a gloss the project has given the same form before. **Enter** takes it, and typing replaces it.
* **Value lists.** On a cell, **Alt+↓** lists every value the project has given that form, with counts.
* **Tagset lists.** A field held to a tagset opens its list on its own and narrows it as you type (see <<_tagsets>>).

On a morpheme field with a lexicon field, such as Gloss, each row of the **Alt+↓** list pairs a value with the lexicon sense it goes with.
Taking one sets the gloss and the link together (see <<_linking_to_the_lexicon>>).

== Vocabulary links

A word or morpheme can be linked to an entry in a lexicon the project links (see <<_vocabularies>>).
The link button on the word or morpheme opens a popover that lists the entries that fit.
The small form beneath a linked word or morpheme is its entry.

Two rows of the popover save repetition:

* **all ×N** links every unlinked occurrence of the same form in the text at once.
* **Analyze every "…" in this text like this** copies the word's analysis onto the unanalyzed words spelled the same.

To draw on the rest of the project, or to have a model make the first pass over the whole document, use **Auto-analyze** (see <<_auto_analyze>>).

== Respelling a word

**Respell** in a word's popover, or **F2** on any of its cells, opens a box over the word with its text selected.
**Enter** saves the new spelling, and **Esc** or clicking elsewhere cancels.
The word keeps its morphemes, glosses, lexicon links, multi-word expressions, and comments.

A word with one morpheme spelled as the word gets the new spelling there too.
A word split into morphemes keeps their forms as they are.

A spelling must have at least one character and no space.
To split a word, use the Tokenize tab, and make larger changes on the Baseline tab.

== Morpheme types

A morpheme's **type**, such as stem, suffix, or enclitic, decides how it joins its neighbours: `=` beside a clitic, `-` otherwise.
In the grid, a clitic's `=` sits against the clitic, with a narrow space toward its host: `ni= house`, `house =ni`.
Copied and exported text writes the plain `=`.

Typing `=` in a morpheme form splits it and types the clitic by its place in the word:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Where the `=` is | Result

| After the word's first morpheme
| The first morpheme is a proclitic.

| Before the word's last morpheme
| The last morpheme is an enclitic. In a word of two morphemes, the second is the enclitic.

| Between two inner morphemes
| Neither is typed. The `=` still shows, in the grid and in every copy and export.
|===

A type chosen by hand, given by an import, or carried by a linked entry is never changed this way.

The **Type** list in a morpheme's link popover sets the type, and **—** clears it.
On an unlinked morpheme it sets the morpheme's own type.
On a morpheme linked to an entry with a type, it shows the entry's type, and a change applies to every use of the entry.
Only writers and maintainers of the vocabulary's project can change an entry's type.

The reference chapter says how each export writes an inner `=`, and what merging, deleting and clearing do to a type (see <<_ref_morpheme_types>>).

== Provenance

Plaid IGT tracks who, or what, produced every value.
The grid distinguishes four states:

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
| State | Looks | What it is

| **Typed by you**
| Plain
| A value a person entered. A marked value you have edited or confirmed keeps only a thin violet underline.

| **Machine-made**
| **Violet italic**
| A value a service proposed, or a vocabulary link Auto-analyze made. It is in the document, and it waits for a person to accept or discard it.

| **Contributed**
| **Amber italic**
| A value entered by someone whose work the project reviews (see <<_access>>). It waits for a verifier to accept or discard it.

| **Guess**
| Faint gray
| A placeholder in an empty cell. It is not in the document at all until you take it with Enter.
|===

A guess on a **pale teal** background comes from the linked lexicon entry.
One on a plain background comes from what the same form was given elsewhere in the project.

Hover over an annotation to see where it came from.
When the service that made a value gave its confidence, hovering shows the percentage.
A value below the project's **Low confidence** threshold gets a heavier, darker underline, and a maintainer sets the threshold on the project's **Services** settings page.

A value that a field's tagset refuses is underlined in red, and a closed tagset will not let it be entered at all (see <<_tagsets>>).

=== Reviewing marked values

Editing a marked value confirms it, so the violet and amber italics disappear as you work through a document.
Three keys do the same a word at a time:

* **Ctrl/Cmd+Enter** accepts everything proposed on a word and moves to the next.
* **Ctrl/Cmd+Backspace** discards a word's proposal.
* **Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+↑/↓** jumps between the words that still have one.

=== Contributors and verifiers

A member whose work is reviewed is a **contributor**.
Everything they enter is marked contributed, including a machine proposal they accept.
Everyone else is a **verifier**, whose work shows plain and whose edits and confirmations settle marked values.

== Copying a sentence

**Copy as IGT** copies one sentence for a paper or a message.
It offers aligned plain text, tab-separated text, LaTeX (gb4e or ExPex), and leipzig.js HTML.

The LaTeX keeps characters like ə and ∅ as they are, so typeset it with XeLaTeX or LuaLaTeX.
Gloss parts written in capitals, such as NOM or 1SG, are set in small caps.

== Saving

A value is saved when you leave its cell, and there is no save button.
When the server cannot be reached, the save status reads **Can't reach the server, retrying**, or **Offline, retrying** when the computer has no network.
Your edits are sent once the server answers again, and closing the tab meanwhile asks first.

On every tab but Analyze the save status is beside the tabs.
A save the server has not answered after three seconds reads **Saving…** there until it lands.

=== Two people in one document

Two people can have one document open at once, but neither sees the other's edits as they are made.
Edits to different fields are all saved.
An edit that meets someone else's change is handled by where the two met:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| The other person changed | What you see

| The same cell, before you
| The cell shows their value with an amber edge and yours under it, and a message names the change, such as `b changed this to DOG.` **Enter** keeps your value, and **Esc** or typing another value lets it go.

| The same field, elsewhere in the document
| Your edit can be refused with **Changed elsewhere**. Your value stays in its cell, not saved.

| Something else your edit clashes with
| The edit is refused, and the document shows the latest version for you to redo it.
|===

= Comments

Any sentence, word, morpheme, or annotation value can carry **comments**: a conversation in the margin of the text, for a question to the speaker, a doubt about a gloss, or a note to a classmate.
In the Analyze tab, hover a sentence's label, a word, or a morpheme and click its comment badge to read the thread or add to it.
A badge with a number marks a thread that already has comments.

The **Comments** tab lists every thread in the document, newest activity first.
Click a thread to open it, and click its arrow to jump to its sentence.
Comments take Markdown.
You can edit your own, and a maintainer can remove any.

A comment stays even when what it was about is edited away, such as a word that is merged or retyped.
It is then listed under the tab's **Outdated** filter until someone deletes it.
Comments are not part of a document's history (see <<_history>>), and are left out of every export but the native archive.

An entry in a vocabulary takes comments too.
Open the entry to read or add to its thread, and the vocabulary's own **Comments** tab lists every thread.

= Vocabularies

A **vocabulary** is a lexicon: a list of **entries** (words or morphemes) that carry their own fields, such as a gloss or a part of speech.
While you analyze, you connect a word or morpheme to an entry, by hand with the link button on the word or morpheme, or in bulk with **Auto-analyze**.
Linking keeps the same word analyzed the same way everywhere it appears.

Every vocabulary belongs to one project, and that project's roles decide who may change it (see <<_a_vocabularys_project>>).
Other projects can link it too, and there it is read only to anyone who is not a member of its own project.

Vocabularies have their own area in the app, under **Vocabularies** in the header, grouped by the project each belongs to.
There you browse and edit entries and define the fields an entry carries.
Which vocabularies a project draws on is part of the project's settings (see <<_text_and_vocab>>).

== Links and entries

A linked entry offers a guess in each field that has a **lexicon field** (see <<_annotation>>): the entry's value in that lexicon field.
So a morpheme linked to an entry glossed `dog` is offered `dog` in its Gloss cell.

Editing an entry changes it for every document linked to it, in every project that links the vocabulary.
A value already in a document's field stays as it was.

In the link popover, the linked entry's form opens the entry in its vocabulary.
When no entry fits, the **+** row under the list adds one with the word's or morpheme's form and links it.
The new entry also takes the values the word or morpheme holds in fields that have a lexicon field, such as its gloss.
Writers and maintainers of the vocabulary's project can add an entry this way, in any project that links it, and nobody else is offered the **+** row.

An entry can carry a morpheme **type**: stem, prefix, suffix, enclitic, and so on, following the FieldWorks inventory.
A linked morpheme takes its type from the entry, or goes by its own when the entry has none (see <<_morpheme_types>>).

[[_a_vocabularys_project]]
== A vocabulary's project

A vocabulary belongs to the project it was made in, which its page names under its title.
That project's members have the same role in the vocabulary:

* **Reader**: reads the entries.
* **Writer**: also adds entries and changes anything stored on one, such as its fields, type, senses and examples.
* **Maintainer**: also renames, merges, deletes and restores entries, changes the vocabulary's settings, and renames, moves or deletes the vocabulary.

**New vocabulary** makes one in a project you maintain, and a new project or an import makes its vocabularies inside the new project.

Another project can link the vocabulary when a maintainer of that project is also a member of the vocabulary's project.
There its members read the entries and link words and morphemes to them, and change an entry only by their role in the vocabulary's own project.

The vocabulary's **Settings** tab lists those projects under **Used by other projects**.
**Unlink** ends one's link and removes its links to the entries, after saying how many there are.

A maintainer of two projects can move a vocabulary from one to the other on its page, and the old project keeps it linked.
A vocabulary cannot be deleted while another project links it, and neither can its project.

== Managing entries

A vocabulary's entries are shown on its **Entries** tab as a table you can search, and sort by clicking a column heading.
A **Uses** column counts the words and morphemes linked to each entry, and an entry's **Concordance** shows every place it is linked.

The search box reads every column, or one field chosen beside it: pick **Gloss** and type `go` to see the entries glossed with it.
With a field chosen, a count of the entries that have nothing in that field appears under the box, and clicking it shows just those.

Writers and maintainers of the vocabulary's project add entries and edit their fields here.
**Enter** in a one-line field, or **Ctrl/Cmd+Enter** anywhere in the entry, saves it.
Only a maintainer of the vocabulary's project renames, merges or deletes an entry.

Three tools work on many entries at once:

* **Bulk Add** takes rows pasted from a spreadsheet or read from a CSV or TSV file.
* **Replace** finds and replaces text in one field of every entry.
* **Export** downloads the table as a tab-separated file that opens in any spreadsheet.

=== Bulk Add

Save an `.xlsx` or `.ods` file as CSV first.
You map the columns onto the entry fields, and each row is checked against the existing entries before anything is created.
A homonym stays separate, and an existing entry is not overwritten unless you say so.

When a file has a title or extra lines above its header, set the row the table starts at.
**The same value on every row** gives every new entry one value in a field, such as a source or a status.

=== Replace

**Replace** matches as plain text, as a whole value, or as a regular expression, and a maintainer can replace in the form too.
A fourth kind, **is empty**, fills the field on the entries that have no value in it.
Each entry it would change is listed with its old and new value, and only the ticked ones are written.

Plain text ignores case, and keeps each match's capitals when both boxes are typed in small letters: `ka` to `ga` makes `Kalamang` `Galamang`.

== Entry fields

Every entry has a **Form**, its reference form or headword, which is required.
Beyond the form, an entry carries a set of fields.
A new vocabulary starts with a few built in:

* **Morph Type**: whether the entry is a stem, a prefix, a suffix, and so on. The editor uses it to draw the joining hyphens on the form and gloss lines.
* **Gloss**: the entry's gloss, offered as a guess in each field whose lexicon field is Gloss.
* **POS**: the entry's part of speech.
* **Definition**: a fuller definition or note.
* **Status**: how far along the entry is, held to a closed list (machine, draft, reviewed, published).

You can add your own fields, reorder them, and remove any except Form, Morph Type, Gloss and Status.
A field marked **inline** also shows on each entry's row in the link popover.
Other fields stay on the entry's own edit form.

An entry's form is stored without affix markers.
Typed or imported as -s with no morph type, it is the suffix s.

=== Roles and languages

Each field's **role** says what its values are: a gloss, a definition, a part of speech, or the entry's status.
The FLEx, CLDF and LaTeX exports write each field by its role, and the assistant reads them.
A field with no role is none of these, whatever it is called.

A field you add is given the role its name suggests, such as Gloss for `Gloss (fr)`.
Several fields can share a role, such as a gloss in each language, and each records its **language**.

=== Tagsets on entry fields

A field can be held to a **tagset** (see <<_tagsets>>), so a part of speech is always `n` and never sometimes `noun`.
A vocabulary keeps its own tagsets, under its Settings, because it is shared across projects.
A closed tagset becomes a dropdown on the entry form and refuses a value outside the list in Bulk Add.
The table counts the entries already outside it, and a click shows only those.

The rules for the Status field, for forms written with affix markers, and for the UMR fields of a vocabulary linked to a UMR project are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_entry_fields>>.

== Headwords and senses

Every vocabulary is a dictionary.
An entry is a headword, and it can have senses under it, each of which can have senses of its own.
A word with one meaning is simply an entry with a gloss.

Numbers say what is what:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Written | What it is

| *adidi*
| A headword that is alone and has no senses. It has no number at all.

| *adidi 1*
| A headword with senses, or the first of several entries spelled *adidi*.

| *adidi 1.1*, *adidi 1.2*
| The senses of *adidi 1*.

| *adidi 1.2.1*
| A sense of the second sense.
|===

On screen the number is a subscript after the form (kai₁), as FieldWorks writes a homograph number.
In exports and in the history it follows a space.

=== Arranging senses

Three buttons on an open entry build the tree:

* **Add sense** writes a new sense directly under it.
* **Set parent** makes any entry a sense of another.
* **Add headword** raises a new entry over the open one, with the same form, and makes the open one its first sense. This is how a word with one meaning gets a second.

**Senses**, beside an open entry, unfolds the whole entry as a numbered tree.
Drag a sense in it to reorder it, onto another sense to nest it there, onto **Make separate entry**, or onto **Move to other entry**.
When several entries share a form, the number after the headword is a button, and dragging in its list renumbers them.

The entry list opens in a **By entry** view that draws senses indented under their entry, beside a **Flat** view.
A sense with no morph type of its own goes by its headword's.

=== References between entries

A field can hold a reference to another entry.
On the Settings tab, set the field's **Type** to **Entry** for one reference or **Entries** for a list, and name it for what it means: *Variant of*, *See also*, *Compound of*.
On the entry form such a field is a picker, and the entry it points at lists the reference under **Referenced by**.

References stay within one vocabulary.
Bulk Add and Replace leave Entry fields alone, since those hold references and not text.

A field can also be set to **Headword only**, so it is offered on a headword and not on its senses: an etymology or a pronunciation belongs to the entry, a gloss to each sense.

=== Examples

Examples are chosen from the texts, never typed in.
**Use as example**, beside each row of an entry's Concordance, adds that sentence to the entry's **Examples** panel, with its translation and a link into the document.
When the word is later deleted or linked elsewhere, the example says so and can be removed.
A sentence worth citing that is not in any text belongs in a document, where it is glossed like any other.

=== Deleting, merging and importing

Deleting an entry makes its senses entries of their own, and clears the fields that named it.
Merging entries with **Bulk Edit** moves their senses and the references to them onto the surviving entry.

A FLEx or CLDF import keeps the sense structure of the lexicon, and so does every export of it.
FLEx's variants and complex forms come in when you tick **Variants and complex forms** on the review screen, as *variantOf* and *components* fields.

== Multi-word expressions

Some lexical units are longer than a word: an idiom, a phrasal verb, a compound name.
You can link such a **multi-word expression** to a single entry by gathering its words:

1. **Shift+click** each word. Or, from one of a word's cells, press **Shift+→** (or **Shift+←**) to take in its neighbours, holding **Ctrl/Cmd** as well to skip a word that does not belong.
2. A blue line shows the words gathered so far. Press **Enter** to link them, or **Esc** to give up.
3. The popover works as it does for one word: pick an entry, or create one. A new entry gets the words joined by spaces, which you can edit to the citation form, and the type _multi-word expression_ (a phrase, to FieldWorks).

A linked multi-word expression draws as a line under its words, labelled with the entry's form.
A skipped word shows as a dotted stretch.
A word can belong to several multi-word expressions at once, and each word keeps its own entry link and morphemes regardless.

Click a line's label to change the entry, unlink it, or accept one that **Auto-analyze** made.
While that popover is open, Shift+click a word to add it to the expression or take it out.

= Auto-analyze

Most of the words in a running text are repeats, and much of the rest can be guessed at.
**Auto-analyze**, a button on the Analyze tab's toolbar, makes that first pass over a whole document so that your time goes into checking rather than typing.
It runs up to four steps in order, each of which you can switch on or off.
Everything it produces shows in violet until you accept or discard it (see <<_provenance>>), and nothing a person has written is ever changed.

== The four steps

1. **Propose translations.** A translation service drafts a free translation for every sentence. It runs first because the later steps read the translation. Needs a connected translation service.
2. **Copy previous analyses.** Every word that has not been analyzed at all is given the analysis the same word form has most often had elsewhere in the project. This is built in and needs no service (see <<_copying_previous_analyses>>).
3. **Propose segmentation and glosses.** An analysis service, such as PolyGloss or a language model, proposes morphemes and glosses for every word a person has not analyzed. Needs a connected analysis service.
4. **Link to the lexicon.** Words and morphemes no person has linked are linked to lexicon entries, by the built-in rule described below or by a service. It runs last so that it can link the stems step 3 produced. Needs the project to have a vocabulary.

A service step is unavailable when no service for it is online.
An analysis service writes its glosses into the morpheme field whose lexicon field is the entry's gloss.
A translation service writes into the sentence field whose role is the translation, or the field chosen in the dialog.
With no such field the dialog asks for one.

== A run

A run over a long document takes minutes.
The dialog closes when the run starts, and a banner at the top of the document lists the steps, ticks them off as they finish, and shows the progress and elapsed time of the step under way.
Editing is paused while the run writes.

A run can end early in three ways:

* **Stop** on the banner ends the run at the step it is on, and what that step wrote stays.
* Leaving the document ends the run after the step under way, and reopening it says which steps did not run.
* Closing or reloading the tab asks first.

When the run finishes, a message says what was done.
Then review the result with the review keys (see <<_reviewing_marked_values>>).

== Copying previous analyses

Step 2 reuses your own work, in the manner of FieldWorks' guesses from previous analyses.
A word receives a copy only when it is **unanalyzed**: still a single morpheme identical to the word, with no annotation values and no lexicon link.
A word you have segmented, glossed, or linked, even partly, is never touched.

For each such word, Plaid IGT looks at the other occurrences of the same form, in the document and in other documents of the project.
It copies the analysis given most often: the morphemes, the lexicon links, and the annotation values.
If two analyses are tied, the word is left alone for you to decide.

Only analyses a person made or confirmed count, so one machine pass never feeds the next.
What a copy carries is set per project under **Settings → Services** (see <<_settings_services>>).

== Linking to the lexicon

The built-in linking rule of step 4 works word by word and morpheme by morpheme:

* A form that has been linked before follows **precedent**: the entry that form has most often been linked to in the project, counting only the links a person made or confirmed.
* A form never linked before is linked to the lexicon entry with the same form, if there is one.
* A whole word is never linked to an affix or clitic entry.
* A run of words whose joined form matches a multi-word expression entry is linked as one (see <<_multi_word_expressions>>).

The rule replaces only links nobody has confirmed.
A link a person made or confirmed stays, and so does an entry an analysis service chose for that occurrence.

TIP: Accept links as you go. An entry a form has been linked to becomes the precedent for every later occurrence of that form.

=== Picking a sense and a gloss together

**Alt+↓** on a morpheme field with a lexicon field, such as Gloss or POS, opens a list of each sense with its value.
The list also holds the alternatives an analysis service weighed, with their probability, and the values the project has given the form.
Choosing a row sets the link and the gloss together.
A gloss typed by hand changes only the gloss.

The reference chapter has the finer rules of each step, under <<_ref_auto_analyze>>.

= Search

A project's **Search** tab looks across every document.
Pick where to look: word forms, morpheme forms, any annotation field at any scope, or the lexicon's entries.
Type what to find, and choose whether a match **contains** it, **is exactly** it, or **matches** a regular expression.

Contains ignores case, and every kind finds text however its accents were typed.
A regular expression is read the same way in Search, in Bulk Edit, in a vocabulary's **Replace**, and by the Assistant (see <<_ref_search>> in the reference chapter).

Results come in two views:

* **Hits** lists every match grouped by document, each in its sentence with the translation. Click one to open that sentence in the Analyze tab.
* **Frequencies** counts the distinct matching values instead. This is how you see every gloss a suffix has been given, or every spelling a word has had.

= Bulk Edit

The **Bulk Edit** tab (maintainers) changes many places at once, the way FieldWorks' Change Spelling does.
You describe the change, preview every place it would touch, tick the ones you mean, and apply them as one action in the history.
There are four operations:

* **Respell words**: change the spelling of a word everywhere it occurs, keeping its morphemes, glosses, links, and other annotations. Optionally respell the same string inside morpheme forms and lexicon entry forms too.
* **Replace in a field**: find and replace across one field's values, as plain text or a regular expression, or across morpheme forms.
* **Re-analyze a word**: give every occurrence of a word the same analysis, chosen from the analyses it already has in the project.
* **Merge lexicon entries**: fold duplicate entries into one, moving every link and reference to the survivor.

Respelling and replacing match the way Search does: a match **contains** the text, **is exactly** it, or **matches** a regular expression.
Contains ignores case, and keeps each match's capitals when both boxes are typed in small letters: `ka` to `ga` makes `Kalamang` `Galamang`.

Some changes are skipped:

* One that would leave a word, a morpheme form, or an entry empty.
* One a closed tagset refuses.
* A word whose new spelling the **Respell** box in Analyze would refuse.
* A place that changed after the preview. The message after **Apply** says how many.

A long preview is paged, and a document's heading ticks every match in that document.
When **Apply** stops partway, the message says what was written, and **Apply** again writes the rest.

Respelling a lexicon entry and merging entries need a maintainer of the project the entry's vocabulary belongs to.
Entries of any other vocabulary are listed and left as they are, as *maintainers only*, or as *read only* when its project is one you cannot open.

= Tagsets

A **tagset** is a controlled list of values for a field: the parts of speech your project uses, the Leipzig abbreviations you gloss with, the genres a document can be.
Tagsets are defined under **Settings → Annotation**, and any field can then point at one.
Several fields can share a tagset, so Gloss at the word scope and Gloss at the morpheme scope agree.
A vocabulary's entry fields take tagsets too, kept with the vocabulary (see <<_entry_fields>>).

== Modes

Each tagset has a mode:

* **Open**: the whole list is offered while you annotate, but anything can be typed.
* **Closed**: only values on the list can be entered. For a fixed inventory such as part of speech.
* **Closed, plus lexical glosses**: a value is allowed if it has a lowercase letter or is in a script without capitals, and anything else must be on the list.

The third mode is made for glosses, where a stem's gloss is a word like `dog` and a grammatical gloss like `1SG.NOM` is built from tags.
A lowercase abbreviation beside a tag in the same gloss is read as a tag, so the `sbj` and `pfv` of `sbj:3.pfv` must be on the list.
The gloss of an affix, a clitic, or a zero morph is never read as a lexical gloss.

== Values and delimiters

A gloss is composite, so a tagset lists the parts and names the **delimiters** that join them (for Leipzig, `.`, `:`, and `>`).
In a governed cell the list opens on its own and offers the part under the caret, and a value the tagset refuses is underlined in red.
A value can carry a description, shown beside it in the list.

**Add values used in this project** fills a new tagset with the values a field already uses, so an existing corpus comes under a tagset without retyping anything.

== Enforcement

A tagset is enforced as you type and in Bulk Edit.
What else a **Closed** tagset refuses depends on where the value comes from:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Source of the value | What happens outside the list

| An approved Assistant plan or a script
| The value is refused.

| An import
| The value is kept, but cannot be confirmed while it is outside the tagset.

| A service's proposal
| The value is kept, but cannot be confirmed while it is outside the tagset. The Validation tab lists it.
|===

Closing a tagset, removing a value from a closed one, or assigning a closed tagset to a field is not saved while the field holds values outside the list that a person typed or an approved Assistant plan wrote.
A **Not saved** notice names those values, with how many annotations use each.
Change them first, or add them to the tagset.

NOTE: The Assistant and the LLM glossing service read a governed field's tagset before proposing values, so they use its spellings.

== Validation

The project's **Validation** tab (maintainers) lists, for each governed field, every value in the project that its tagset refuses, with a count.
It offers the remedies: **Add to tagset**, **Fix in Bulk Edit**, or open each occurrence and correct it by hand.
A field under an **Open** tagset is listed too, with the values that closing it would refuse.

Validation also finds zero morphs written some other way than ∅ (see <<_the_zero_morph>>).
The rarer cases it reports, such as a tagset that is out of force on a field, are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_tagsets>>.

= Guidelines

The **Guidelines** tab is the project's own annotation manual: the conventions the people working on it have agreed to, written down where everyone can read them.
Tagsets say which values a field takes.
Guidelines are for what a list of values cannot say: that loanwords are not segmented, that a free translation is idiomatic rather than word-by-word, that this project follows Leipzig except for the ergative.
Everyone who can open the project can read them, and anyone who can annotate can write them.

Each guideline has a title and a body written in a rich-text editor (bold, headings, lists, tables, links), and **Markdown** switches to the source.
Two habits keep a manual useful:

* Name the subject rather than the rule, so that the title still fits after the rule is refined. If a title cannot name what a guideline covers, it is two guidelines.
* Open the body with the rule itself, since that first line is what the Assistant sees when the manual is too large to send whole.

== Guidelines and the Assistant

Pinning a guideline sends it to the Assistant in full with every question.
Unpinned guidelines are sent in full while the manual is small, and the Assistant opens them by title once it is large.

The Assistant can draft a guideline as well as read one.
If you tell it a convention while asking about something else, it will propose writing it down, and you approve or discard it like any other change it proposes.
When it changes a guideline that already exists, the card shows the passage it is replacing and what with.

== Record and archive

Every change to a guideline is recorded in the project's Activity, with who made it and when.
Guidelines travel in the Plaid IGT archive and come back on import, so a project handed to somebody else arrives with the conventions it was annotated under.

= History

Every change in Plaid IGT is recorded in an **audit log**, with a meaningful message and the identity of the person who made it.
Past states of any document can be viewed, read-only, with each vocabulary entry as it read at that moment.
Comments are not part of the history, and neither is the recording itself, so a past state cannot bring back a deleted recording.

== A document's history

Open a document's history with **History** at the end of its tab row, or from the rail at the left edge of the editor.
Each entry is one action as you performed it, such as "Merge morphemes" or "Tokenize".
An edit made in a cell names the field, the word or morpheme, the sentence and the value, such as `Gloss of "dogs" in sentence 3: DOG`.

Selecting an entry shows what it changed (see <<_changes_in_an_entry>>), and **Document** above it shows the document as it was right after that action.
An entry made of several changes expands to show each one.

Actions performed by a service show up the same way, under the service's name and naming the person who ran it: `LLM translation (12 sentences), requested by Ana`.
A change made through an API token names the token too.

Some entries are marked with their kind:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Mark | The entry is

| **Import**
| a file brought in.

| **Automatic**
| a run of a service or of a built-in step, such as Auto-analyze's.

| **Assistant**
| an approved Assistant plan.

| **Job**
| an assistant job's work (see <<_a_jobs_history_entries>>).

| **Bulk edit**
| a change made in Bulk Edit or a vocabulary's **Replace**.

| **Guess taken**
| a faint guess accepted.

| **Review**
| a proposal accepted as it stands.

| **Repair**
| a repair of the document's data, rather than an edit.
|===

== Restore

To bring a document back to an earlier state, select that entry and click **Restore**.
Everything in the document goes back to how it was at that moment, except the recording.
A dialog lists what will change before anything is written, and says what cannot come back, such as a field deleted since.

The restore is one more entry in the history, and the message that confirms it offers **Undo**.
Restoring is for a project's maintainers.

== Undo

To take back what one entry did, and nothing else, click **Undo** over its changes.
Each value, word, morpheme and link it wrote in the document goes back to how it was just before the entry.
The undo is one more entry in the history, which can itself be undone.
Undoing is for a project's maintainers.

A dialog lists what will be undone before anything is written.
What cannot go back is left as it is, and the dialog lists it with the reason and where it is:

* A value changed again later is left.
* So is anything something was added to since, such as a word that now has a gloss.
* Lexicon entries are not undone from a document. Undo the same entry in the vocabulary's History instead (see <<_vocabulary_history>>).
* Changes to settings, fields, vocabularies and members are never undone.

Suppose a service glossed fifty words and you have since corrected three of them.
Undoing the service's entry takes back the other forty-seven and leaves your three as they are, each listed with its reason.

[[_changes_in_an_entry]]
== Changes in an entry

An entry opens on the sentences it changed, in document order.
Each is drawn interlinear, with the value after the entry above the one it replaced, struck through.
A word segmented anew shows its old morphemes struck through on a line under the new ones.

Each value carries its usual mark, so a machine-made, contributed or confirmed value reads as it does in the grid.
A person's value the entry replaced is struck through in red.

The count above the sentences says how many changes the entry made and how many replaced a person's value.
**A person's value replaced** shows only the sentences where one did.
Below the sentences come the document's name and metadata and the lexicon entries the entry created, changed, merged or deleted, each a link.

The address of the page names the entry, so it can be shared.
What counts as a person's value replaced, and how a very large entry is drawn, are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_history>>.

== Vocabulary history

A vocabulary has a history of its own, opened with **History** beside the vocabulary's name.
It lists every change to the vocabulary and its entries, but not links, which belong to each document's history.
**History** on an open entry lists only the changes to that entry.

Selecting a history entry shows what it changed: the entries it created, changed, merged or deleted, before and after, and the links it moved, removed or made, counted per document.
**Vocabulary** above it shows the vocabulary's entries as they were at that point.

To put one entry back as it was, open it in the past state and click **Restore**.
An entry deleted since comes back whole, but without its links in documents.

**Undo** over a history entry's changes takes back what it did to the vocabulary's entries and to every link to them, in any document.
An entry it merged or deleted comes back with its links, and an entry it created goes.
As in a document, the dialog lists what is left and why.

Restoring an entry and undoing are for maintainers of the vocabulary's project.

= Activity

A project's **Activity** tab, for maintainers, shows who has been working and on what.

The upper table counts each person's **changes** over a window you choose.
A change is one action, so importing a corpus counts once, not once per sentence.
**By services and jobs** counts the ones among them that a service or an assistant job made at the person's request.
Each row also counts the documents the person touched, with a bar for each day of the chosen window (the last 30 days for All time).
Below it, the members who have made no changes in that window are named.

The lower table is the project's history as a feed, newest first, each row naming the person, what they did, and the document it happened to.
Changes made in this project's vocabularies, such as merging, renaming or deleting entries, appear here.
Changes made in a vocabulary linked from another project appear only in that vocabulary's History and in that of each document they changed.

For one document's history, and for putting a document back the way it was, see <<_history>>.

= Assistant

The **Assistant** tab is a chat with a language model that knows your project.
Ask it what is still unglossed, whether a suffix is glossed consistently, or to summarize the morphology it can see.
It reads the documents, the lexicon, the search results, the project's guidelines, and the history to answer, and it shows what it read.

A line above each answer sums up the work behind it, such as "read 87 sentences in 40 documents · 2 searches · 42 steps".
Opened, it lists each step the assistant took, and a step opens to show exactly what it read.

== Plans

When you ask the assistant to change something, it does not touch the data.
It proposes a **plan**, which you approve or discard.
The plan lists every change under the document or lexicon it lands in, and each word links to its place in the editor, so a change can be checked before it is approved.

Two marks point out the changes to look at first:

* **Rewrite** marks a change that rewrites your baseline text. Deleting a sentence is a rewrite: its text goes, with its words, their analyses and links, and its translation.
* **Accepted** marks a change to something a person made or accepted.

The buttons on a plan decide how its values are recorded:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Button | What it records

| **Approve as unverified**
| Every value the assistant writes is its proposal, unconfirmed, as a service's proposals are. You confirm them later in the editor.

| **Approve as verified**
| The values are the assistant's work, accepted by you.

| **Approve**, with **Record as human-made** ticked
| The values are your own work.

| **Discard**
| Nothing is written.
|===

Applying a plan needs Writer access.
Changes to a lexicon need the matching role in the lexicon's own project (see <<_a_vocabularys_project>>).
The assistant does not change a lexicon linked from another project, so ask it from the lexicon's own project instead.
The remaining rules of approval are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_assistant>>.

A plan that cannot be applied as it stands says so on its card:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Card | What happened

| **Out of date**
| A sentence the plan changes has been edited since it was made, and the plan is refused. Ask again to have it planned on the current text.

| **Partly applied**
| The plan stopped partway. The assistant can finish it.

| **Nothing to write**
| The plan only keeps values as they are. It can only be discarded.
|===

== Conversations

Conversations are private to you, though your server's administrators can read them.
They are kept per project, listed on the left of the tab, and each has its own link.
**All projects** widens the list.
A conversation can be renamed with the pencil beside it, downloaded or copied as Markdown, downloaded as a web page, or deleted.

A reply lands whether or not the page stays open, so you can leave, reload, or close the browser and find the answer when you come back.
**Stop** ends a turn early, even while the model is still working on its answer, and **Retry** sends the question again.

A conversation is worked on in one tab at a time.
Opened in a second tab it is read-only, and **Continue here** moves it to that tab.

== Privacy and the model

Where the assistant's model runs, and where what you send it goes, depends on the Plaid site you use.
When the people running the site's assistant have said, the panel shows their statement at the start of a new conversation and behind the **About this assistant** button in its header.
Otherwise, ask the site's administrators.

The assistant picker names who runs each assistant.
An assistant run by a maintainer of the project or by an administrator answers every member.
One run by anyone else answers only the person who runs it.

== Other projects

Where the assistant allows it, **Add project**, beside the paperclip, lets a question read other IGT projects you can open, to compare or to look something up there.
Each project added is shown above the message box.
The assistant only reads them, and plans changes in the conversation's own project alone.
A citation into another project carries that project's name.

== Files and PDFs

The paperclip in the message box attaches up to five files to a question, and dropping or pasting a file does the same.
The assistant reads tables (`.csv`, `.tsv`), text (`.txt`, `.md`), JSON, XML, `.flextext`, `.eaf` and `.lift` files, and PDFs.
Reading a `.flextext`, `.eaf` or `.lift` file does not bring it into the project. Import it for that.
A reply can also give you a file the assistant made, such as a cleaned table, to download.

To add many lexicon entries from a table, load it with **Bulk Add** as it is, then ask the assistant to tidy the new entries: merge repeated forms, normalize values, mark where they came from.

A PDF must have text in it, so a scan is refused.
Ask about "§9" or "page 41" and the assistant reads that part.
Text taken from a PDF can come out wrong where the PDF's fonts are old, so check a form against the PDF before relying on it.
Where the assistant can look things up on the web, it also reads a PDF from a link or a DOI pasted into a question.

== The panel

The **Assistant** button in the header opens the same assistant in a panel beside whatever you are looking at, as does the handle on the right edge of the window.
The panel stays open as you move around, and it can be dragged wider or narrower.
It picks up the last conversation you had in the project you are in and keeps it as you move from document to document.
The panel's history lists your other conversations, and **Open in Assistant** moves the open one to the **Assistant** tab.

A question asked on a document is about that document, unless it names another.
Two controls point a question at something:

* **Ask**, beside **Copy** on a sentence, puts that sentence into the question.
* Typing **@** in the message box names a sentence of the open document, an entry of the vocabulary you are on, or a document of the project.

Sentences are matched on what they say, so **@** followed by a word finds the sentence that contains it.

== Conversation size

The panel's header shows how full the conversation is, and hovering over the bar gives the details.
A conversation fills up as it grows, because every turn sends the whole thread again.
Past 85 percent the bar turns amber.
A full conversation sends nothing more, and **New conversation** beside the message box starts one with what you had typed.

== Assistant availability

The assistant is a service that your server's operator runs and picks the model for.
In a project with none connected, the tab, the **Assistant** buttons, the handle and **Ask** are not shown.
On the project list the button is shown whether or not an assistant is connected, and the panel says so when the project you pick has none.
Conversations already saved still open from their own links.

= Assistant jobs

An **assistant job** has the assistant work through documents on its own, by written instructions, with nobody at the screen.
A job can fill a word field such as a part of speech, segment and gloss words, write translations, make a value consistent across texts, or check the documents and report what it finds.
It writes its changes as it goes and ends with a report of what it did.
It goes on after you close the browser.

== Setting up a job

A job is set up by asking the assistant for one, in any conversation.
The **Assistant** button beside **New document** on the **Documents** tab, and on the **Jobs** tab, opens the assistant as the button in the header does.

Say what the job should do and on which documents, such as filling Word class in Texts 3 to 9, or translating every sentence that has no translation.
The assistant reads the documents, says how much is empty of what you asked for, and asks about what it finds there, such as a form people analyzed in two ways or another field that is empty.
It can also do the first few sentences as an ordinary plan for you to correct, and the job then follows what you approved.

The assistant then drafts the job as a card in the conversation, headed **Proposed job**:

* **Documents**: the documents the job works, or **All documents** with their number.
* **Fills**: what the job may fill, such as segmentation, a field or an orthography, or **Nothing** for a job that only checks or counts.
* **Text**: **Unchanged**, or **May be edited** when the job may retype, split, merge or delete words and sentences.
* **Instructions**: what the job's model reads with every part of its work, at most 2,000 characters. You can edit them on the card.

To change what the job fills or whether it may edit the text, tell the assistant, and it drafts a new card.
**Start job** on the card starts the job, and **Discard** drops it.
Starting a job needs Writer access.

The job is planned to fill only what its card names, and edits the text only when the card says it may.
A value written outside what the card names is not refused, and the report marks it **not in the setup**.

A project has one job at a time, and starting another while one has not ended is refused.
What else the card and the job's page say is in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_setting_up_a_job>>.

== Starting without the assistant

**Start without the assistant**, in the **⋯** menu on the **Jobs** tab, opens the **Start job** form, which starts a job with no conversation.
It asks for the documents, **All documents** or **Selected documents**, and the **Instructions**.
**Translate** and **Check** fill the instructions with a starting text to edit.
The job decides from the instructions what it fills and whether it edits the text, and its plan shows on its page.

== Changes a job makes

A job writes every change it plans in its documents and in the project's lexicons, in the name of the person who started it.
That covers values, analyses, links, comments, lexicon entries, and deletions.
Three things about its writing matter most:

* **It can change and delete what people wrote or confirmed.** The report counts such changes under **People's work**.
* **Everything it writes is machine-made and unverified** (see <<_provenance>>), also where it replaces a person's value. It shows in violet italic until a person confirms or edits it.
* **It edits the text only when its plan says the instructions ask for that.** The plan on its page then ends **Text may be edited.**

A job that fills something linked to a lexicon links words and morphemes to entries, and creates an entry where none fits (see <<_machine_draft_entries>>).

A job never deletes a document.
Some changes it does not write but lists in its report under **Proposed changes**, for a person to make:

* a change to the project's setup: a guideline, a tagset, a document's name or metadata, or a new document,
* a change in a document other than the one being worked,
* a change over the whole project, a confirmation, and a restore of a document,
* renaming, merging or deleting an entry when the person who started the job is not a maintainer of the project.

Editing stays open while a job works in a document.
When someone writes in a document while the job works on it, the job drops what it was about to write there and puts the document off.
When it comes back, it works the document as it then reads, and may change what they wrote.

When it finishes, the job makes its own analyses consistent.
A form it analyzed in more than one way takes the analysis people gave that form, when they all gave the same one, or else the analysis the job gave it at least two times in three.

The finer rules, such as what an analysis replaces and when a job changes an existing entry, are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_what_a_job_writes>>.

[[_machine_draft_entries]]
== Machine draft entries

An entry a job creates has the Status **Machine draft**, and a person's edit of it leaves that status as it is.
An existing entry a job edits keeps its status.

On a vocabulary's **Entries** tab, **N machine drafts** lists only those entries, with any search on top, and its address can be shared.
While it is on, two buttons work on the whole list:

* **Mark reviewed** sets the status of every listed entry to reviewed.
* **Delete**, for a maintainer of the vocabulary's project, deletes them all with their links, and leaves the glosses.

A job's report links to each lexicon's machine drafts.
An entry a job created that ends with no links is listed in its report under **Entries with no links**, for a person to delete.
A job deletes such entries only when its instructions say so.

== Planning the work

A job first plans how to cut the work, and the **Plan** line on its page says how from the moment planning ends:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Plan line | How the job works

| **Worked in stretches of sentences**
| A few sentences at a time. The plan may take only the sentences with something empty of what the job fills, or only the sentences matching a search.

| **Worked one document at a time**
| A whole document in each part. A document whose part fails, or is too long for one part, is worked in stretches of sentences instead.

| **Worked on all the documents at once**
| One step over every document, which is the job's only step. What it leaves empty stays empty.
|===

The line then names what the job fills, such as **Fills: segmentation, Morph Gloss.** or **Fills nothing.**
Each part of the work is done by the assistant with the project's guidelines, the instructions, and what the planning added to them, which the page shows as **Assistant's instructions**.

When planning ends with no plan, the job fails having written nothing, and the Plan line says why.
The variants of each plan line are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_planning_the_work>>.

== Following a job

The project's **Jobs** tab lists its jobs, newest first, with their state, instructions, number of documents, who started each, and when it was queued and ended.
Each opens to the job's page.

A job's page has its state at the top, and under it a row of sections, each with its own address: **Overview**, **Documents** and **Setup** while the job waits or runs, and more once it has ended (see <<_the_report>>).

A new job is **Waiting** until the job worker takes it, and it shows how many jobs are ahead of it.
A job that runs is **Running**, with what it is doing beside it: **Checking**, **Planning**, **Working**, **Making analyses consistent** or **Writing the report**.
A line under it says where it is, such as **Story B: sentences 1 to 5 of 17. 3 steps in this part.**

**Documents** is a table of the job's documents in the job's order, with each one's state and how many of its sentences are done.
Besides **Waiting**, **Working** and **Done**, a document can read:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| State | Meaning

| **Deferred**
| Someone wrote in it while the job worked on it or in the last ten minutes, so the job put it off. A document still in use after three tries ends **In use**.

| **Stopped partway**
| The job ended after doing some of it.

| **Not reached**
| The job ended before it.
Once the job has ended, **To check** says why: **Not reached: the job was stopped** or **Not reached: the job failed**.

| **Skipped**
| Every sentence the job took was skipped.

| **Nothing found**, **Left out by the plan**
| The plan did not work it.

| **Deleted**
| The document was deleted.
|===

While the job runs, each document's **Changes so far** opens what the job has written there until now.
A document the job is working in shows a banner, **Assistant job**, with its progress.

**Work so far** on the Overview lists each part of the work as it finishes, newest first, with the assistant's note on it.
The notes are the assistant's own words, not checked against the counts.

The reference chapter lists everything else the page shows, and the ways a job can fail, under <<_ref_following_a_job>>.

== Stopping a job

**Stop**, on the job's page or on the banner, is for the person who started the job and for maintainers.
A waiting job ends at once.
A running one reads **Stopping…** and ends after the sentences in hand.
What it wrote stays, the page says **Stopped by** whom and when, and the job has its report like any other.

== The report

Once a job has ended, its **Overview** gives its instructions and what it was set up to fill, then its report.
The report has four parts, in this order:

1. **One sentence on what the job changed**, such as **Changed 1,354 sentences in 129 of 130 documents.** It also says when the job worked in one step, was stopped, failed or was undone, or changed nothing.
2. **To check**: what needs a person, a row for each kind with how many there are. **Show** opens exactly what the row counts. It reads **Nothing.** when nothing does.
3. **Totals**, in two groups, **Written** and **Not written**.
4. **Assistant's summary**: the assistant's account of the job, marked **Written by the assistant, not checked against the counts above.**

Under the opening sentence, the job's own line says where it changed what people wrote or the text:

* **People's work: 33 values, morphemes or links people wrote were changed or removed. What the job wrote in their place is unverified.**
* **Text: 25 words added, 2 existing words or punctuation marks changed or removed.**

Each list of the report has a section of its own beside **Overview**, shown only when it holds something, with its count beside its name:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Section | What it lists

| **Blank**
| Every word, morpheme or sentence still empty of what the job fills in the sentences it wrote in, linked into the editor.

| **Skipped**
| Each sentence the job skipped, with why.

| **Proposed**
| What the job did not write, for a person to make.

| **Entries**
| The entries the job created, a link to the machine drafts in each lexicon, and **Entries with no links**.

| **Respelled**
| Words a text edit respelled that kept their analysis, so their morphemes may still spell the old word.

| **Left out**
| The documents the plan did not work.

| **Tables**
| Tables the job counted.

| **Made consistent**, **Left inconsistent**
| The forms whose analyses the job made consistent, and those it left, with why.
|===

**Documents** also has a **To check** column with why each document needs a person, and **To check** above the table shows only those documents.

A job that fills nothing, such as a check, shows its **Findings** first in its report, in full, with **Copy**.

Every row of the totals, every reason a sentence is skipped, and every kind of refused change is listed in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_the_report>>.

== A job's history entries

Each document's History has one entry for the job's work in it, named **Assistant job:** with the first words of its instructions.
Where the job made analyses consistent there is a second one, named **Assistant job: analyses made consistent**.
Both are marked **Job**.

On the job's page, a document's **Changes** and **Analyses made consistent** links in **Documents** open those entries on what the job changed there (see <<_changes_in_an_entry>>).
So does a document's name in **Blank** and **Skipped**.

== Undoing a job

Once a job that wrote something has ended, **Undo job** on its page, for the project's maintainers, takes back what it wrote in every document and in the lexicon, as one entry in the history.
The dialog, **Undo this job?**, lists what will be undone under **To undo** and, under **Left as they are**, what people changed since, with the reason and the place, before anything is written.
A value changed again later is left, and so is anything something was added to since.

The job then reads **Undone**, and its page says **Undone by** whom with the time and **Show in History**.
A job's undo cannot itself be undone.

A job of more than 20,000 changes is undone document by document, from each document's **Changes** link, and each of those undos is an ordinary entry in the document's history, which can itself be undone.
Restoring a document to the moment before the job (see <<_history>>) also takes back the job's work in that document, but not in the lexicon.

== Discussing a job with the assistant

**Discuss with the assistant**, on the page of a job that has ended, opens the assistant panel on a new conversation about that job.
It is offered where an assistant is online and the window has room for the panel.
The assistant reads the job's report and tells you what the job did.
A job's record holds what it did, the model's note on each part of its work, and nothing more of why.

The report is as the job ended, so the assistant reads the documents as they are now before it plans a change, and you approve the change as any other plan.
This is how a job's proposed changes are made: ask the assistant for the ones you want.
From any page, the assistant can also list the project's jobs and read their reports.

== Limits

* One job worker serves the whole server and runs one job at a time, so a job waits behind the jobs of other projects.
* A job can take hours, as it takes turns with people using the same AI model.
* A job takes at most 500 documents, so a larger project starts one on a selection.
* A job writes nothing in a sentence of more than 250 words or 4,000 characters.
* What a job writes is a draft: every value is unverified until a person reviews it, and the assistant's summary and notes are not checked against what it did.

== The job worker

Jobs run only while the server's operator runs the job worker, `plaid-job-worker`, with an AI model.
When no worker is running, a job waits, and the **Start job** form and the job card say **No job worker is running. The job waits until one starts.**

= Services

Plaid IGT can call external **services** for certain tasks.
A service is a small program, often a Python script wrapping a model, that connects to your project when it starts and advertises which task it handles.
The AI assistance described in the introduction is connected this way.

Once connected, a service appears wherever its task is offered, and a maintainer can make it the default for that task under **Settings → Services**.
Some tasks ship with a built-in method that needs no service at all.
Whatever a service produces is marked machine-made, in violet italic, until a person confirms it.

== Tasks

There are six integration points:

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
| Task | Where | What it does

| **Tokenization**
| **Tokenize** tab
| Splits the baseline into words. The built-in splitter is always available. A service can split sentences as well (the bundled example uses Punkt).

| **Transcription (ASR)**
| **Media** tab
| Transcribes and time-aligns a recording. There is no built-in, so this needs a connected service (the bundled example uses Whisper).

| **Speech detection**
| **Media** tab
| Proposes where the utterances are in a recording. The built-in runs in your browser (see <<_speech_detection>>).

| **Translation**
| **Auto-analyze**, step 1
| Proposes a free translation for each sentence. The bundled example uses a language model.

| **Analysis**
| **Auto-analyze**, step 3
| Proposes a morpheme segmentation and glosses for every word. There is no built-in. Three examples are bundled: PolyGloss, CWoMP, and a language model given the project's own analyses as examples.

| **Auto-link vocabulary**
| **Auto-analyze**, step 4
| Proposes vocabulary links for unlinked words and morphemes. The built-in rule follows your project's own precedent.
|===

The Assistant is a service too (see <<_assistant>>).

== A service run

Every service run works the same way.
A button in the header of the panel its results land in opens a dialog with the **Method** to use, that method's options, and the button that starts the run.
The dialog closes when the run starts.

A run that writes to the document makes it read-only until it finishes, and only one such run goes at a time.
A banner at the top of the document shows the run on every tab: its progress, what it is doing and how long it has been going.
**Stop** on the banner ends a run, and whatever it wrote stays.

A run also outlives the page that started it: reopen the document and the run is picked up where it got to.
Speech detection writes nothing, so it can run while you work, and its dialog shows its progress and **Stop** instead of the banner.

The messages a stopped run leaves are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_services>>.

== Services of your own

Writing a service of your own is not hard.
The link:manual.html#_services[Services chapter of the Plaid manual] explains how.

= Import and Export

== Importing

Start a project from existing data on the **New project** screen.
Five formats are supported:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Format | What comes in

| **FieldWorks (FLEx) backup** (`.fwbackup`)
| A whole FLEx project: its texts, words, morphemes, glosses, translations, the full lexicon, and what each text's Info tab holds.

| **FieldWorks (FLEx) interlinear texts** (`.flextext`)
| One or more files exported from FLEx with **File > Export Interlinear**, with their texts and analyses but no lexicon.

| **CLDF** (`.zip`)
| A Cross-Linguistic Data Formats dataset. Its examples become interlinear texts, its lexicon a vocabulary, and its language the project's identity.

| **ELAN** (`.eaf`)
| A set of annotation files in which each tier means the same thing in every file. Each file becomes a document, with its tiers, speakers, time alignment, and its recording when you choose that alongside.

| **Praat** (`.TextGrid`)
| Text files saved from Praat, imported the way ELAN files are (see <<_importing_from_praat>>).

| **Plaid IGT archive** (`.zip`)
| A project recreated exactly, with its vocabularies, media, comments, and guidelines.
|===

What each format carries and leaves out is in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_importing>>.

=== Importing from FLEx

A `.flextext` holds only what FLEx's Interlinear view showed, so turn on every line you want before exporting.
An analysis FLEx marks as a guess arrives as machine work to review.

A FLEx backup keeps the times of sentences that were timed against a recording, and they become the segments on each document's Media tab.
To bring the recordings too, choose them together with the backup, and each goes to the texts that name it.

A FLEx part of speech has a name in each analysis language, `v` in English and `kt.kerja` in Indonesian, and only one of them is imported.
Choose which under **Part-of-speech language** on the review screen.

=== Importing from ELAN

The review screen of an ELAN import has three parts, in the order you work:

* **Files** lists each `.eaf` with the document it becomes and its recording under it.
* **Tiers** is where each tier is given its role. A tier is matched across files by its name, so files need not have the same tiers in the same places.
* **Preview** draws a sentence as the interlinear lines the import would write, and redraws it as you change a role.

The roles a tier can take:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Role | What the tier becomes

| **Sentences**
| The transcription. Each annotation becomes one sentence, and their text, joined in time order, becomes the document's text. Every import needs one such tier.

| **Words**, **Morphemes**
| Tiers below it, dividing each sentence and then each word. Without a Words tier, each sentence is split into words as **Tokenize** splits it.

| **Morpheme type**
| Each morpheme's type in FLEx's terms (`stem`, `suffix`, `enclitic`).

| **Time alignment**
| The segments on the Media tab. Leave it out and the sentence tier's own times are used.

| **Orthography**
| An alternate spelling of each word, beside the baseline.

| **Sentence field**, **Word field**, **Morpheme field**
| One value per sentence, word, or morpheme, in an annotation field.
|===

ELAN files can also be imported into a project you already have, with **New document → Import from ELAN or Praat** on its Documents tab.
Each tier is written into a field the project already has, or into a new field of its own.

[[_importing_from_praat]]
=== Importing from Praat

A TextGrid goes through the same review screen as an ELAN file, and can be chosen together with ELAN files and with its recording.
A recording is matched to the TextGrid of the same name, such as `Story.wav` to `Story.TextGrid`.

A TextGrid's tiers are flat, so the import reads which tier belongs under which from their times, comparing each tier with the tiers before it in the file:

* A tier whose intervals have the same start and end as those of an earlier tier holds one value for each of them, as a translation tier does for a transcription tier.
* A tier whose intervals lie inside those of an earlier tier divides it, as a forced aligner's word tier divides the utterances.
* Any other tier stands on its own.

Only intervals with text are read. A point tier has nothing to import and is listed with no role.
A tier named `name@Speaker` belongs to that speaker, as in ELAN.
A binary TextGrid is refused: save it from Praat as a text file.
Two tiers of one name come in as `words` and `words (2)`.

A file imported before says so on its row, and you choose whether to keep that document, replace it, or add a copy beside it.
Replacing deletes the document before its replacement is written, so a run that fails partway may leave a file with neither, and says so.

=== Unfinished imports

An import that stops before it finishes, whether you stop it, it fails, or the tab is closed, leaves a project that does not open.
Wherever it would open, it reads **This import did not finish. Delete it and import again.**, with **Delete** for a project maintainer.
**Delete** asks for the project's name, and deletes the project with the vocabularies the import made in it.

== Exporting

Exports are driven by **presets**.
A preset fixes a format and which orthographies, fields, and options it includes, so the same export can be run again later, or by someone else.

Maintainers create presets on the project's **Export** tab.
Anyone can run one from there or from a document's Export tab, on this document, selected documents, or the whole project.
An export holds everything its preset names, or it fails, and the export panel says what could not be read.

Seven formats are supported:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Format | What goes out

| **Plain text**
| An aligned, human-readable interlinear rendering.

| **FLEx** (`.flextext` + `.lift`)
| The texts as FieldWorks' interlinear XML together with the project's vocabularies as a LIFT lexicon, zipped together.

| **CLDF TextCorpus** (`.zip`)
| A Cross-Linguistic Data Formats dataset, one CSV per table with a `cldf-metadata.json` describing them.

| **ELAN** (`.eaf`)
| One annotation file per document, bundled into a `.zip` with its recording when it has one.

| **Praat** (`.TextGrid`)
| One TextGrid per document, bundled into a `.zip` with its recording when it has one. Only time-aligned sentences go out (see <<_exporting_to_praat>>).

| **LaTeX book** (`.zip`)
| LaTeX source for a book of the texts, to compile with LuaLaTeX (see <<_latex_book>>).

| **Plaid IGT JSON** (`.zip`)
| A lossless archive you can import again later. Only this format carries comments.
|===

To grab a single sentence instead, use **Copy as IGT** in the Analyze tab (see <<_copying_a_sentence>>).
The options of each format are in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_exporting>>.

=== Exporting to FLEx

FieldWorks has to receive the lexicon before the texts, because the texts link to entries that must already be there:

1. Import the lexicon from the Lexicon area (**File > Import > LIFT Lexicon**), keeping the `.lift-ranges` file beside the `.lift`.
2. Import the texts from the Texts & Words area (**File > Import > FLExText Interlinear**).

A README in the `.zip` repeats these steps.

A FLEx preset also carries the writing-system tags FieldWorks will see, for the baseline text, each alternate orthography, and glosses and translations.
Use the codes your FieldWorks project already uses, or FLEx will offer to add a new writing system when you import.

[[_exporting_to_praat]]
=== Exporting to Praat

A TextGrid holds only what has a time, and in Plaid only segments have times.
So each tier holds one interval per sentence, timed by the segments inside it, and a sentence with no segment is left out.
Words and morphemes have no times of their own, so each word line is written whole in its sentence's interval:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Tier | Text of one interval

| **Sentence** | `nzone yəf rä`
| **Words** | `nzone yəf rä`
| **Morphemes** | `nzone yf rä`
| **Gloss** | `1SG.POSS name 3SG.FEM:SBJ:NPST:IPFV/be[stem]`
| **Translation** | `My name is`
|===

Each word line has one item per word, so the third item of **Gloss** belongs to the third item of **Words**.
A word with no value is written `_` to keep the count.
A **Segment** tier holds the segments when some sentence has more than one, and is empty otherwise.
Put the recording beside the TextGrid and open both in Praat, then select them together and click **View & Edit**.

=== Exporting to CLDF

CLDF has no place for vocabulary links, the marks that tell machine work from a person's, or most morpheme types.
These are left out, and the preset lists them before the export runs.

[[_latex_book]]
=== LaTeX book

A LaTeX book export turns the documents in its scope into a book of interlinear texts: a title, a table of contents, a list of abbreviations, and one chapter per document.
Each sentence is a numbered example with the lines the Analyze tab shows, then the free translation and the other sentence fields.
The preset's **Example lines** and **Sentence fields** lists turn each line on or off and set their order.

With **Vocabulary chapter** on, the book ends with the project's vocabulary, either the entries the texts link to or every entry.
In the PDF, each linked word in the examples links to its entry.

To make the book, unpack the `.zip` and compile `main.tex` with LuaLaTeX through latexmk:

----
latexmk -lualatex main.tex
----

Run by hand, LuaLaTeX has to run twice, or the table of contents stays empty.
An online LaTeX editor that compiles with latexmk takes the `.zip` as it is, with its compiler set to LuaLaTeX.

What the `.zip` holds, and how the book is typeset, is in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_latex_book>>.

= Settings

A maintainer opens a project's **Settings** tab.
It has five sections, listed down the left side.

== General

The project's name, its languages, the **Research** and **Project plaid** switches, and the option to delete the project.
Deleting a project deletes its vocabularies with it, and is refused while another project links one of them.

Two languages are recorded: the **object language**, which the baseline text is in, and the **meta language**, which glosses and translations are written in.
Each has a name, an ISO 639-3 code, a Glottocode, and a **writing system tag**, the label FLEx and ELAN files carry on their text (`oni`, `pmy`, `en`).
An import from FLEx, ELAN, or CLDF fills these in from the file.
The languages go out with exports such as CLDF, where a dataset without a Glottocode cannot be linked to any other.

The two switches:

* Under **Research**, **Record responses to suggestions** records when a suggestion is shown to a member, accepted or replaced, and when an assistant plan is expanded. It is off by default.
* Under **Project plaid**, **Show the project's plaid** draws the project's plaid beside its name and in the browser tab.

== Text and vocab

Three things live here:

* The **additional orthographies**, such as IPA, another script, or a normalized form. Each is a row of its own under every word in the Analyze grid, empty until filled in by hand or by a tool (see <<_additional_orthographies>>).
* Which **vocabularies** the project draws on (see <<_vocabularies>>). The project's own are always linked, and **New vocabulary** makes another. A vocabulary of another project can be linked by a maintainer who is also a member of that project.
* The project's **special characters**: the backslash codes that type characters your keyboard lacks (see <<_typing_special_characters>>).

== Annotation

This section holds the annotation **fields** at each scope, sentence, word, and morpheme.
You can add, reorder, and remove them, and removing a field first tells you how many values it holds.

Each field records a little about itself, which exports, guesses and services read:

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
| Setting | On | What it says

| **Language**
| Every field
| The language its values are in, as a writing system tag such as `pmy` or `nl`. FLEx and ELAN exports label the field with it.

| **Lexicon field**
| Word and morpheme fields
| The entry field that holds the same kind of value, such as Gloss for a gloss field. Guesses from a linked entry and the list of senses under **Alt+↓** read it.

| **Role**
| Sentence fields
| The translation, the literal translation, a note, or none. Exports read it to know which field is the free translation, and services write their translations into that field.
|===

A field with no lexicon field takes nothing from the lexicon.
An import fills all three in, and a new field is given what its name suggests:

* A field named with a tag in parentheses, such as `Gloss (nl)`, is given that tag.
* A field named like an entry field, such as `Gloss` or `POS`, is given that entry field.
* A sentence field named `Translation`, `Literal Translation` or `Note` is given that role.

**Tokenize new text**, on by default, splits the text added on the Baseline and Media tabs, and by the assistant, into words.

This section also holds the project's **tagsets** (see <<_tagsets>>) and the **document metadata** fields shown on every document's Details tab.

=== Ignored tokens rule

The **ignored tokens** rule says which tokens carry no word-level annotation and host no morphemes, usually punctuation.
It has two forms:

* **Unicode punctuation** takes every punctuation mark and symbol, with a few exceptions such as ∅ and the tone letters.
* **Explicit list** instead takes only the tokens you list, separated by commas.

Under **Unicode punctuation**, the **characters that behave as letters** are the project's own exceptions, such as the apostrophe of an orthography that writes a glottal stop with one.
Each listed character joins the word around it instead of splitting it, and stays on the word's form in the lexicon.

The built-in exceptions are listed in the reference chapter, under <<_ref_ignored_tokens_rule>>.

== Access

The project's members and their roles, invitation links, and API access.
See <<_sharing_and_permissions>>.

**Review work**, a checkbox per member, marks everything that person enters as contributed, in amber, until a verifier confirms it (see <<_provenance>>).
It is independent of the role: tick it for a community member or a student whatever access they have, and leave it off for a trusted collaborator.

[[_settings_services]]
== Services

Which service, or built-in method, each task uses by default: tokenization, transcription, speech detection, translation, analysis, and auto-linking (see <<_services>>).
A default can carry default options too, and people can still switch per use.

Each task also has **default fields**, the fields a service writes into or reads, such as the gloss field an analysis service fills and the translation it reads.
Setup sets them from the fields it makes.

The **Copy previous analyses** card says whether Auto-analyze ticks its copy step by default.
It also says what a copy takes from a word's previous analyses: its segmentation, its links, its field values, or any combination.

= Sharing and Permissions

Projects are shared by adding members, each with a role:

* **Reader**: can view everything but make no edits, comments included.
* **Writer**: can edit and delete documents, link vocabulary, and add and edit entries of the project's vocabularies.
* **Maintainer**: can also change project settings, manage members, and delete the project and its vocabularies.

Add and manage members under the project's **Settings**, in **Access**.
Members are found by name or by email address.
When you only have Reader access, the editor shows everything, but a banner says so and its controls are inert.

== Invitation links

**Invitation links** let people join without an administrator creating each account.
A maintainer mints a link that grants a role on the project, good for a set number of uses and a set number of days.
Whoever opens it chooses their own password and lands in the project.
Someone who already has an account signs in from the link instead and joins the project with the link's role.

A link is not addressed to anyone, so record who it was meant for in its private **Label**.
A link can be revoked at any time, and it stops working if its maker loses the access it grants.

An **administrator** can also create accounts directly from the same Access section, each added to the project with the role chosen, and can mint a password-reset link for someone who is locked out.

== API tokens

Scripts and services should not borrow your sign-in.
Mint a named **API token** on your profile page instead.
It can be revoked on its own, and its name appears in the history.
A token cannot manage accounts or passwords, create or revoke tokens, or make invitation and password-reset links.

= Typing Special Characters

Most language data needs characters a keyboard does not have.
In any field that holds language data, type a backslash followed by a two-character code, and the code turns into the character as you finish it:

[cols="1,1,2"]
|===
| Code | Character | Name

| `\sw` | ə | schwa

| `\ng` | ŋ | eng

| `\?g` | ʔ | glottal stop

| `\ae` | æ | ash

| `\:f` | ː | length mark

| `\'1` | ˈ | primary stress

| `\00` | ∅ | zero morph (see below)
|===

The codes are Praat's, so if you already use Praat they are the ones you know.
There are over four hundred of them, and the full list is under **Settings → Text and vocab → Special characters**.
For anything not in the list, `\u0250` and the like give any character by its Unicode number.
Type two backslashes for a plain backslash.

Codes work in the Analyze grid, the baseline, the transcript, lexicon entries, and document metadata.
They do not apply where a backslash has to mean itself, such as a regular expression in Search or Bulk Edit.

TIP: If you type a lot of one language, a system keyboard such as SIL Keyman is worth setting up. It works everywhere on your computer rather than only here.

== A project's own codes

Every code is the project's to change.
Under **Special characters**, each code is a row with its **Code**, its **Character**, a **Note**, and when the project **Last modified** it.
Search for a code to see what it types, and click a column heading to sort by it.

Point a code at a different character, delete it, or add a two-character code of your own, then **Save**.
A code that is already in the list says what the existing one types, and **Show** brings that one into view.
A change applies to everyone working on the project.

The reset button beside a changed code puts it back as it ships.
A deleted code leaves the list and stops working, and **Restore built-in codes** brings back every deleted one.

== The zero morph

A morpheme with a grammatical job and no sound is written with the empty set sign, ∅.
It has a code of its own (`\00`) and a shortcut in the Analyze grid: **Alt+0** in a morpheme form types it.
Write it as ∅ rather than a capital O with a stroke or a zero, so it exports as a zero morph.
The Validation tab lists any morpheme that looks like a zero written another way.

= Reference

[[_ref_baseline]]
== Baseline

A later edit of the baseline follows these rules, beyond the ones under <<_later_edits>>:

* When you select two or more words and type or paste over them, letters you type again at either end are kept only as whole words. Select _cat sat_ and paste _c_, and both words go.
* A paste or a retype that covers a whole sentence or segment and more text deletes it, with its translation or its times.
* A segment whose exact text you replace keeps its times.
* A new line typed after a sentence joins that sentence, and one typed before the first sentence becomes a sentence of its own.
* To make a new line a sentence, **Ctrl/Cmd+click** its first word on the Tokenize tab.
* When a space splits a word, the part holding more of the word keeps its analysis, and the other part is a new word with none.
* Punctuation the ignored tokens rule splits words at, typed at the start or end of a word on the Baseline or Media tab, stays out of the word.
* **Ctrl/Cmd+Z** in the box undoes typing, and **Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z** redoes it.

[[_ref_tokenize]]
== Tokenize

* A letter is never split from its combining marks, and an emoji joined with U+200D stays whole.
* A sentence split that hides a multi-word expression, or shows only part of it, asks first. Merging the sentences again shows it whole.

[[_ref_media]]
== Media

=== Mixing one file per speaker

In the **Mix recordings** dialog:

* Each row takes the speaker of that file and an offset in seconds. The offset is silence put before the file, for a file that was started late, and a negative offset trims its start.
* A stereo file is averaged to one channel first.
* **Full quality** keeps the highest sample rate among the files, **Smaller** resamples to 16 kHz, and each says the size it comes to.
* The dialog starts on **Full quality**, or on **Smaller** when only that one fits the server's limit.
* A mix the server would refuse is refused here, before anything is uploaded.
* A file the browser cannot decode is marked **Cannot be decoded.**, and its **Remove** takes it out of the mix.

=== One file with a speaker on each channel

A file chosen alone under **Per speaker** is not mixed:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| File | What is uploaded

| A WAV
| The file as it is.

| Any other audio file
| A WAV with the same channels, at **Full quality** or **Smaller** as a mix is.

| A video file, a WebM or MP4 that holds only sound included
| Nothing. It is refused: upload it under **Single**.
|===

**Remove speakers per channel** under the list makes the recording a plain recording again.

[[_ref_morpheme_types]]
== Morpheme types

=== Clitic boundaries

Each `=` typed is kept with the morpheme after it.
The types are worked out again over the whole word each time its morphemes are split, merged, or deleted, so the result does not depend on the order they were typed in.

What takes a `=` away:

* Merging the two morphemes beside it, or deleting the morpheme after it.
* Deleting a word's first morpheme, for the `=` after it.
* Clearing a clitic type in the **Type** list, for the `=` that gave it.

Clearing any other type keeps the `=`, and a `=` the cleared type blocked then types the morpheme at once.

What carries a `=`:

* A copied analysis (Analyze every, Auto-analyze, Bulk Edit) carries each `=` with it.
* A CLDF import types the pieces of a word written with `=` by the same rule.

In the grid, a `=` that is not a proclitic's or an enclitic's sits in the middle.

=== Clitic boundaries in exports

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Export | How a `=` is written

| FLEx
| An inner `=` is written in front of the morpheme after it, as FLEx writes an enclitic. A proclitic's `=` is written once, on the proclitic. Before a morpheme typed as anything but a clitic, such as a stem or a suffix, none is written, since FLEx has no way to say it.

| ELAN
| Every `=` is written in front of the morpheme after it.
|===

=== Type list

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| The morpheme is | The list shows and sets

| Unlinked
| The morpheme's own type.

| Linked to an entry with a type
| The entry's type. A change applies to every use of the entry.

| Linked to an entry with no type
| The morpheme's own type, not the entry's.
|===

While the form of the **+** row is open, the list reads **Type (new entry)** and sets the type of the entry being created:

* It starts on the type the list showed before (the linked entry's, else the morpheme's own), or on **—** when there is none.
* An entry created as is takes that type.
* A form typed with a marker, such as -s, sets the list to the type the marker names, unless a type was picked from the list.
* Closing the create form with **Esc** brings back the list of the linked entry or the morpheme.

In a project with no lexicon, the **set type** button under a morpheme opens the list.

[[_ref_vocabularies]]
== Vocabularies

=== Link popover

On a word or multi-word expression that no entry is spelled like or starts with, the popover starts on the **+** row.

=== A vocabulary's project

When a project is unlinked from a vocabulary:

* The unlink is listed in that project's Activity and in the vocabulary's History.
* Morphemes there that took their type from an entry keep it when you can write that project, and have none otherwise.

Under **Used by other projects**, a project you cannot open is listed without its name.

[[_ref_entry_fields]]
=== Entry fields

**Forms and affix markers.**
An entry's form is stored without affix markers:

* Typed or imported as -s with no morph type, it is the suffix s.
* Given a morph type, as a FLEx import or the entry form gives one, it keeps that type: a bound stem written theŋjən- is the bound stem theŋjən.
* A form with spaces inside its markers, such as -du, du-, is kept as written.

**Status.**

* Every vocabulary has a Status field, and its role, its type and where it is shown cannot be changed.
* The value machine, shown as **Machine draft**, marks an entry an assistant job created (see <<_machine_draft_entries>>).
* The values machine and published are reserved: neither the tagset editor nor Replace renames or removes them.

**Roles.**
A new vocabulary's Gloss, POS, Definition and Status fields hold their roles, and an import gives its fields theirs.

**UMR.**
An entry in a vocabulary linked to a UMR project also has a **UMR** band, below its fields:

* **Roleset** is the concept the entry stands for on a UMR graph, such as `lunch-01`. Left empty, the entry stands for its headword.
* **Arguments** describe the roleset's roles, one row each: a name (`ARG0`, `ARG1`, and so on) and a description (`the one eating`).

[[_ref_auto_analyze]]
== Auto-analyze

=== Copying previous analyses

Besides the open document, step 2 reads up to 25 other documents of the project, those with the most words it is looking for.
A document of more than 10,000 words is read only when there are not 25 shorter ones.

=== Linking to the lexicon

The built-in rule replaces only links nobody has confirmed:

* its own,
* links copied with an earlier analysis,
* links to an entry that no longer has the word's or morpheme's form.

On a word step 2 copied, the rule keeps the links the copy brought and links only what has none.

In a project with more links than the server counts at once, the rule links a word or morpheme only when the lexicon has exactly one entry of its form, and Auto-analyze says how many it did not link.
The entries in the **Alt+↓** list are then ordered by the open document's links alone.

=== Picking a sense and a gloss together

A click on the morpheme's link opens the same list as **Alt+↓**, for one field.
That field is the first of these the project has:

1. the field whose values an analysis service gave with their entries,
2. the field whose lexicon field is the entry's gloss,
3. the first morpheme field with a lexicon field.

What a chosen row writes:

* A row with no entry leaves the link as it is.
* A sense with no gloss leaves the gloss as it is.
* Chosen in the popover, a sense keeps a value a person wrote that is not the linked sense's own, and fills the other fields with a lexicon field that are empty or hold an unconfirmed proposal.

[[_ref_search]]
== Search

=== Accents

* Every kind of match finds text however its accents were typed.
* An accent searched for on its own, such as a tone mark, also finds it inside a letter typed with it, so a lone acute finds the one in `á`.
* In a regular expression the accent may also be written as an escape, such as `\x{301}` for the acute, and `\p{M}` finds every accent, inside letters too.

=== Regular expressions

A regular expression is read the same way in Search, in Bulk Edit, in a vocabulary's **Replace**, and by the Assistant.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Written | Meaning

| `\w`
| A letter, mark, digit, or underscore in any script.

| `\b`
| The edge of a word.

| `\p{IsArabic}`
| Any character of the Arabic script, and likewise for every script.

| `(?i)` at the start
| Ignore case.

| `$1` in a replacement
| The first group.

| `$&` in a replacement
| The whole match.
|===

A replacement cannot hold a backslash, so an accent goes in as the character itself.

[[_ref_tagsets]]
== Tagsets

=== Changes that are not saved

* For a composite value, the **Not saved** notice also names the parts that are not on the list.
* When a value is written in the moment before the save, a message names it instead.
* A change to one tagset is never held up by the values of a field that another tagset governs, nor by a field it is out of force on.
* A save of tagsets or fields, in the project or a lexicon, made while another maintainer changes the tagsets is not saved, and says **Tagsets changed elsewhere. Reload and redo your change.**
* So is an approved Assistant plan of tagset changes, and one that assigns a tagset deleted or renamed since it was made.

=== A tagset out of force

A closed tagset is out of force on a field when values a person typed were already outside its list as the tagset reached the field.

* Validation names the tagset and the field above the list.
* The tagset can still be changed, and each save names the values still in the way.
* Once those values are changed, **Put in force** applies the list.
* Saving any tagset or field does the same for every field whose values allow it.

=== Fields with more than one annotation

Validation also names two kinds of field:

* a field that allows more than one annotation on a word, morpheme or sentence, such as a field a script made,
* a field that holds a list no closed tagset names, such as a field whose tagset was deleted.

Under the field it lists each word, morpheme or sentence that holds more than one annotation, with the document and the values, and a link to it in Analyze.
Ignored tokens, which have no fields in Analyze, are not listed or counted.
Analyze shows one of the values at a time, and clearing it shows the next.

Two buttons settle such a field, and neither changes an annotation:

* **Put in force** applies one annotation on each once no word, morpheme or sentence holds two.
* **Remove the list** removes the list. A list whose tagset was made closed again in the meantime stays, and the notice says so.

[[_ref_history]]
== History

=== A document's history

Selecting one change of an expanded entry shows what that one change did, with the document right after it under **Document**.

=== Undo

A merge of a lexicon entry another project borrows moves links in that project's documents too.
Those you cannot open are left as they are, and the dialog says how many, such as **2 changes in documents or lexicons you cannot open are not undone.**

=== Changes in an entry

What is marked:

* Each morpheme's values are shown apart, so a gloss written beside another in the same word is marked on its own.
* Only what the entry itself wrote is marked: a value another change wrote on a morpheme the entry made reads as it is.
* A person's value counts as replaced when the entry deleted it, changed it, or wrote it again as a machine's.
* A change to the text is shown as old and new text and is never a person's value replaced.
* A word or sentence the entry added or deleted shows as such, and a sentence someone changed after the entry says **Changed again later**.

The counts:

* The count above the sentences says what the changes are (values, morphemes, words, links).
* Below the sentences are counts of changes the sentences do not show: to transcript segments, to other annotations (those of other apps, and changes the grid draws no difference for), and to annotations deleted later.

Large entries:

* Every change in an entry is read before it is drawn, with a count of those read so far.
* The sentences are drawn 50 at a time, and **Show more sentences** draws the next.
* An entry too large to draw reads **Too many changes to show here.**, with **Show document** for the document as of it.

A job's page links each document to its entries.

=== Vocabulary history

Links are not counted for an entry with too many changes in documents to count.

[[_ref_assistant]]
== Assistant

=== Plans

* A change over the whole project, such as replacing a gloss everywhere, is one row with its counts, and **Show where** lists the documents.
* A confirmation in the plan is made under either button.
* A plan in which nothing can be marked verified or unverified offers a single **Approve**, for example one of only confirmations, deletions, renames or settings changes.
* If your work is reviewed in this project, the second button reads **Approve as your contribution**, and what you approve with it waits for a reviewer, as anything you enter does.
* Once a plan with two buttons is applied, its card says how it was approved, such as **Approved as unverified**.

=== Plans that change a lexicon

* Adding a lexicon's entries and changing their fields needs a writer of the lexicon's project.
* Renaming, merging or deleting them needs a maintainer of it or an administrator.
* The assistant does not change a lexicon linked from another project, whatever your role in the lexicon's own project.

=== Turns and the panel

* After a turn that failed, **Retry** continues from the steps that turn had done.
* On a window too narrow for the panel, a job's page and an open lexicon entry have an **Assistant** link to the **Assistant** tab instead, and a document's header the assistant's mark beside **History**.
* An answer that ends while you are in another tab or window is announced with a short sound, a ● at the start of the tab's title, and a notification if you allowed them when you first sent a message.

== Assistant jobs

[[_ref_setting_up_a_job]]
=== Setting up a job

The **Assistant** button beside **New document** opens the **Assistant** tab when the window is too narrow for the panel.

A job that may edit the text can be held to fill only the words it adds, and its card then says **Fills only the words this job adds**:

* A word the job types over whole counts as a word it adds, and so does the second half of a word it splits. A respelled word does not.
* The limit covers values, analyses and links on words and sentences, not lexicon entries.

What the card and the page show:

* When the assistant drafts a new card, the earlier card reads **Replaced**.
* Once started, the card is headed **Job**, shows the job's state, such as **Waiting** or **Done**, and links to the job's page with **Open job**.
* The job's page says what it was set up for from the start, such as **Set up to fill Word class. Text unchanged.**, with **Open conversation** for the person who started it.
* A second job is refused with a line such as **This project already has a job (Waiting), started by Ada.** and **Open it**.

On the **Start job** form:

* No document is chosen under **Selected documents** until you choose.
* **Select shown** selects every document the search above the list shows.

[[_ref_what_a_job_writes]]
=== Changes a job makes

How an analysis is written:

* An analysis that keeps a word's morphemes, as many and with the same forms, writes only the values that differ. Every other value and link stays as it was, with who wrote it.
* An analysis of another shape replaces the word's analysis.
* The sentences a text edit changed are taken once more, as the edit left them, to fill what is empty there, the words it added among them.

What a job may do to lexicon entries depends on how it is worked (see <<_planning_the_work>>):

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Worked | Existing entries

| A few sentences at a time
| Never changed.

| A document or all the documents at once
| The job can change an entry's fields. It can rename, merge or delete entries when the person who started it is a maintainer of the project.
|===

A change in a document other than the one being worked is proposed and not written.
When the job works all its documents at once, that means a document outside the job.

[[_ref_planning_the_work]]
=== Planning the work

Variants of the **Plan** line:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| The line adds | When

| **only those with something empty**
| The plan takes only the sentences with something empty of what the job fills.

| **1 in smaller parts**
| A document whose part failed, or was too long for one part, was worked in stretches of sentences. The line counts such documents.

| **Worked on 2 documents in one part**
| A plan over all the documents named only some of them. The others are left out by the plan.

| **then in stretches of sentences**
| The one step over all the documents failed. It is not asked again, and each document is worked in stretches of sentences.

| **Text may be edited.**
| The job may edit the text.
|===

A word, a morpheme or a sentence is empty for a field when the field holds no value on it.
A word is empty for segmentation when it has no morpheme analysis.

**A plan limited to a search.**
A plan in stretches of sentences can be limited to the sentences matching a search, such as every sentence with a given gloss:

* The Plan line names the search and counts what it matched, such as **Worked in stretches of sentences, only the sentences matching Gloss "be.3SG": 33 sentences in 24 documents.**
* The match is taken once, when the job is planned, so a sentence that matches only later is not worked.
* In the table of documents, a document with no matching sentence reads **Nothing found**, with **0 of 20, none found by the plan's search**.
* Another reads like **3 of 3 matching, 17 not found by the plan's search**.

**One step over all the documents.**
Nothing more is worked after the step:

* Only a write put off while a person edited, a sentence a person changed meanwhile, and the sentences its text edits changed are taken again.
* Each document it covered is done, and its row reads **Worked in the one step**.
* The step may not have read every sentence, and the report's first line then says so, such as **The job worked on all 45 documents in one step and changed 24 of them. It did not read every sentence.**

**No plan.**
The Plan line says why planning ended with no plan, such as **Planning ended with no plan. Nothing was written.** or **The assistant's model failed during planning. Nothing was written.**
A job set up from a card ends the same way when every field its card names has been deleted.

[[_ref_following_a_job]]
=== Following a job

**Waiting and failing.**

* A waiting job shows how many jobs are ahead of it, such as **2 jobs of other projects ahead**.
* When the worker has not reported for two minutes, the page says **No worker has reported since** and the time, and the job goes on once a worker runs again.
* A job taken up again this way ten times ends **Failed**: **Its worker stopped during it ten times.**
* A job whose AI model's provider refuses its key or its model ends **Failed**: **The model’s provider refused the job’s calls**, with the provider's words.
* While someone else's assistant or service run uses the same AI model, the job waits its turn, and its line ends **Taking turns with people using the model.**
* When no worker is running, a stop ends the job at once, and the report says **Stopped with no worker running.**

**Overview**, while the job runs, gives:

* its instructions and what it was set up to fill,
* **Documents done** of how many,
* when it started (or **Waiting since**) and how long it has run,
* its plan once planning has ended,
* **Work so far**.

**Setup** gives:

* the plan and the assistant's instructions to each part of the work,
* the number of documents and who started the job,
* when it was queued or started (**Waiting since**, **Started**), how long it waited, when it ended and how long it has run,
* the AI model.

**Documents** lists the job's documents in the job's order (**#**).
It can be searched by name and sorted by its other columns, and columns of counts, such as **Changes written**, appear as the job writes.

**Work so far and Notes.**
**Work so far** on the Overview, and the **Notes** section once the job has ended, list each part of the work as it finishes, newest first, 25 to a page:

* A row gives its document and sentences, linked into the editor, or **All documents**.
* It gives when the part ended and how many steps it took, and what it wrote.
* It gives the sentences it skipped (**Skipped:**) and the changes refused (**Not done, refused:**).
* It ends with the assistant's note on the part.
* A part after a text edit reads **after the text edit**, or **the words added** for a job that fills only those.
* For a job that fills nothing, **Show findings** on a row shows what that part found.

Once the job has ended, **By document** lists the parts by document instead, in the job's order.
When the job ends while its page is open, a page of **Work so far** past the first opens the same page of **Notes**.

[[_ref_the_report]]
=== The report

**The top of the report.**

* The state line says when the job ended, how long it took and who started it.
* The instructions show their first two lines, with **Show all** for the rest.
* A job that worked all its documents in one step has the line **The job worked on all 45 documents in one step and changed 24 of them.**
* When that one step ended before its work was done, the report says so, with how many documents the step did not get to, such as **Its one step ended unfinished and did not get to 2 documents.**
* A job that wrote and proposed nothing says so in one line under the opening sentence, in place of the summary.
* A long **Assistant's summary** is folded, with **Show all**.

**Links to entries the job deleted or merged.**
An entry the job deleted or merged takes or moves every link to it, in any document.
The links people made in this project's documents count under People's work, each in the row of its document.
The line says how many of them are outside the job's documents, such as **1 of them outside the job's documents**.

**To check** has a row for each of these, when there are any:

* documents in use and not worked, not reached, stopped partway, left out by the plan, or not worked because the server did not answer,
* sentences skipped, by why, those skipped and not listed, and those too long and not listed,
* places left blank,
* values people wrote that the job changed or removed,
* documents whose text the job changed,
* changes refused,
* words respelled,
* changes proposed or not written,
* forms left inconsistent,
* entries with no links.

A job that core ended before its report was written lists only the documents in use, not reached, stopped partway or not worked because the server did not answer.
A row of **To check** on the Overview opens the **Documents** table on that one reason, such as **In use** or **Stopped partway**.

**Totals.**
Each row is shown when it counts something:

* **Documents done**, with how many the job changed when it worked them in one step.
* Apart from them, **Documents with nothing found by the plan's search**, **Documents skipped**, **Documents in use**, **Documents deleted**, **Documents not reached**, **Documents left out by the plan**, **Documents not worked, the server did not answer** and **Documents stopped partway**.
* **Sentences written in**.
* **Changes written**, with what they are, such as **6 (3 values on their own and 3 word analyses holding 5 morphemes and 5 values)**, and **Deletions**.
* **People's values, morphemes and links changed or removed**.
* **Changes to the text**, with **Words added** and **Existing words or punctuation marks changed or removed**.
* **Translations written**, **Translations replaced** and **Words analyzed**.
* A row for each field with the values set in it, such as **Gloss** with **8 values (7 in word analyses)**. A field the job's card did not name reads **not in the setup**, and **Values not in the setup** adds them up.
* For each thing the job fills, how much it left blank in the sentences it changed, such as **Gloss left blank in sentences the job changed** with **3 words**. Then how much is blank in the sentences the job did not change, did not reach, and skipped.
* **Lexicon links made**, **Entries created**, **Changes proposed**, and the sentences skipped by reason.
* How long the job spent **Taking turns with people using the model**, **Waiting for people to finish editing**, and on **The model not answering**.

**Sections.**
What each section holds beyond its summary under <<_the_report>>:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Section | Detail

| **Blank**
| In each document, the list is headed **Left blank in sentences the job changed:**, and each place says what it is blank for. Then, in the totals' words, how much is blank in the sentences the job did not change, did not reach, and skipped. 25 documents to a page.

| **Skipped**
| Headed **Skipped sentences**. The reasons are **Too long**, **Changed during the job**, **Write interrupted**, **No answer from the assistant**, or **Failed** with what failed, such as **Failed: answer too long** or **Failed: refused by the model**.

| **Proposed**
| Headed **Proposed changes**. Each has **Where**, **Change**, **From** and **To**.

| **Respelled**
| Headed **Respelled, analysis kept: check its morphemes**.

| **Left out**
| Headed **Left out by the plan**.

| **Made consistent**, **Left inconsistent**
| Headed **Analyses made consistent** and **Forms left inconsistent**.
|===

**Refused changes.**
Under a document in **Blank**, **Not done, refused:** lists the changes the job could not make, which wrote nothing:

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Line | Why

| **Change to words the job retyped, split, merged or deleted**, **Retype, split, merge or delete of words the job also changed**
| One part both reshaped words and changed them.

| **Change that uses an entry the job made**, **Change that uses an entry the job deleted or merged**
| The change needed an entry that the same part created, deleted or merged.

| **Link inside a multi-word expression the job made**
| As the line says.

| **Analysis of a word in a sentence the job retyped**
| The same part retyped the sentence.

| **Value on a word or sentence the job did not add**
| The job fills only the words it adds.

| **Outside the part's own sentences**
| The change was in a sentence the part was not given.

| **Change in a sentence too long for a job**
| The sentence is over 250 words or 4,000 characters, and a job writes nothing in it.
|===

**Findings and undone jobs.**

* The **Findings** of a job that fills nothing are marked **Written by the assistant, not checked against the counts below.**
* A job that is undone says so in its state line and its opening sentence.
* Its **To check** list keeps only the changes it proposed and never applied, and its totals read **What the job had written**.

== Import and Export

[[_ref_importing]]
=== Importing

**FLEx backup.**

* The backup's writing systems become the project's languages and each field's language, so a later FLEx export is tagged the way FLEx expects.
* Words that cannot be aligned to their baseline are listed before the import and skipped.
* A text whose recording is missing or too large is imported with its times. Upload the recording on its Media tab afterwards.

**CLDF.**
A text's recording is the first audio or video file the dataset lists for it.

**Plaid IGT archive.**
Which members' work is reviewed is not carried, since it names people.

**ELAN recordings.**

* A file that names several audio recordings, one per speaker, has those chosen mixed into one recording with a channel each, as under **Per speaker** on the Media tab, at the quality chosen under **Files**.
* When one of those chosen is a video, nothing is mixed: the first one chosen, in the file's order, is the recording, and the others are not used.
* A time origin set on a recording in ELAN is applied, so the segments stay on the speech they were aligned to.

**ELAN tiers.**

* A Words tier's words are kept as they are in the file, punctuation included. Without one, the words are split as **Tokenize** splits them, with punctuation left out of the words.
* Tier names such as `Translation-gls-nl`, as a corpus prepared for FieldWorks names them, are read for their kind and language, so such a tier is offered the matching field.
* A file made by ELAN's **Import FLEx** is read the same way, with its fields named as a FLEx import names them.
* When several tiers hold the sentences, a tier that names no participant takes its speaker from its name: `W-Spch` and `K-Spch` are speakers W and K.

**ELAN into an existing project.**
The documents a failed run finished stay.
**Retry import** with the same choices leaves them as they are, counted as already imported, and makes the rest.

**Unfinished imports.**

* A vocabulary the import made does not open either, and cannot be linked into another project, until the import is deleted.
* **Delete** is refused while another project links one of those vocabularies.
* A lexicon of another project you chose to import into stays, with the entries the import added to it.
* Nothing deletes an unfinished import on its own, since the import may still be running in another tab.
* An import that finished but could not be marked done says so on the import screen, with **Try again**.

[[_ref_exporting]]
=== Exporting

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Format | Options and limits

| **Plain text**
| **Word line** prints the words as written, segmented into morphemes, or both, which is the four-line hand-in a field methods course asks for. **Number sentences** numbers them. **Document header** prints the document's name and metadata above its text.

| **ELAN**
| **Segment words into morphemes** decides whether the tiers reach below the word. **One tier set per speaker** gives each speaker their own tiers. A segment that crosses a sentence break is left out, and the export says how many.

| **Praat**
| **Word tiers** writes the words as written, segmented into morphemes, or both. **One tier set per speaker** gives each speaker their own tiers, and without it a sentence that overlaps the one before is left out. With **Include media files** on, the TextGrid lasts as long as a WAV or MP4 recording. Otherwise it ends at the last segment. The export says how many sentences it left out.

| **Plaid IGT JSON**
| A project with two vocabularies of one name is refused: rename one first.
|===

Which field goes where in a new preset:

* Each field goes out under its own language where the project knows it, and the box beside each field in a FLEx preset overrides it.
* A new FLEx or CLDF preset sends the sentence field whose role is the translation as the free translation.
* A new CLDF preset sends the sentence field whose role is a note as the comment, and the morpheme field whose lexicon field is the entry's gloss as the gloss.

[[_ref_latex_book]]
=== LaTeX book

**Typesetting.**

* Each text begins on a new page under its name, and a punctuation mark is set with the word it belongs to.
* The lines of an example print every character as it was typed, `+--+` as two hyphens included.
* The translation and the other sentence fields are set as prose, with curly quotation marks and `+--+` as a dash.
* Glosses written in capitals, such as `PL` or `1SG`, are set in small caps.
* A document written right to left is set right to left.
* **Document metadata** lists each document's metadata under its chapter heading.
* The book uses the Charis SIL font, with a Noto font for any other script the texts are written in.
* The look of each line, such as italic words, is set by the `+\Plaid...+` commands at the top of `main.tex`.

**Abbreviations.**
The list of abbreviations names every one the texts use, with the description its tagset gives it or the meaning from the Leipzig Glossing Rules, and a blank to fill in where there is neither.

**Vocabulary chapter.**
Each vocabulary, and each of its fields, can be switched off.
Each headword shows its form, its number, its gloss and fields, the examples promoted to it, and then each sense.

The `.zip` holds:

* `main.tex`: the book, with a marked place for front matter such as a title page, preface, and acknowledgments.
* `abbreviations.tex`: the list of abbreviations.
* `texts/`: one file per document.
* `vocabulary.tex`: the vocabulary, when the book has one.
* `latexmkrc` and `README.txt`: the compiler setting and how to compile.

[[_ref_ignored_tokens_rule]]
== Ignored tokens rule

**Unicode punctuation** takes every punctuation mark and symbol except:

* ∅,
* pictographs such as ★,
* the tone letters such as ˥ and ˩,
* the emoji skin-tone modifiers.

Tone letters and skin-tone modifiers are letters in every project, so `ma˥˩` keeps its tone on its form in the lexicon.
