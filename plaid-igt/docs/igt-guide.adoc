= Introduction

**Plaid IGT** is a web-based editor for building interlinear glossed text (IGT).
All documents are grounded in a single baseline text.
This text is segmented into words and morphemes, which then receive customized annotations, such as glosses or parts of speech.

Plaid IGT is one of many apps built on the Plaid platform.
You don't need to know anything about Plaid itself to use the editor, but if you're curious about the underlying platform, or you want to script against your data, see link:manual.html[the Plaid manual] and the link:api-py.html[Python] / link:api-js.html[JavaScript] client references.

[NOTE]
====
Plaid IGT is a *demonstration app*.
It covers the core interlinear-glossing workflow, and summarizes the heavier surfaces (audio media, FieldWorks import) in their own sections below.
====

= Getting Started

Plaid IGT is served by your Plaid server. There is nothing to install.
Open the editor in your browser at the `/igt/` path on that server.
If you are running Plaid locally with the default settings, that is:

----
http://localhost:8080/igt/
----

Sign in with the email address and password your project administrator gave you, or with the password you chose when you opened an invitation link (see <<_sharing_and_permissions>>).
Your session stays signed in until the token expires or you log out.

The editor has a few levels you move between:

* **Projects**: the list of all projects you can access (your home screen after login).
* **Documents**: the texts inside one project. Beside the document list sit the project's own tabs: **Search**, **Assistant**, and **Export** for everyone, and **Bulk Edit**, **Validation**, and **Settings** for maintainers.
* **A document's editing tabs**: `Metadata`, `Baseline`, `Media`, `Tokenize`, `Analyze`, `Comments`, and `Export`, switched with the tabs at the top of an open document.

The Analyze and Media tabs each have a **?** button that opens a legend of their gestures and keys, and this guide is one click away under **Guide** in the header.

= Projects

A project holds a collection of documents along with the settings they share: the metadata and annotation fields, the orthographies, the tagsets, the vocabularies it draws on, and its members.
Create a project from the Projects screen, starting blank (a short wizard walks you through those settings) or from imported data (see <<_import_and_export>>).
Click any project to open its documents.

Each document is built up through a handful of activities, one per tab in the document editor: **Metadata**, **Baseline**, **Media**, **Tokenize**, **Analyze**, and **Comments**.
Much of what these activities offer is configured per project, so two projects can present very different fields and orthographies on the same underlying editor.
The rest of this section walks through each activity.

Interlinear glossed text is organized at three scopes, which the annotation fields and the Analyze grid are built around:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Scope | What it is

| **Sentences**
| Spans of the baseline text. They carry sentence fields such as the free translation, the speaker, and notes.

| **Words**
| The tokens within each sentence.

| **Morphemes**
| The sub-word pieces of each word. Glosses and the other morpheme fields live here.
|===

A word is divided into one or more morphemes.
A simple word is a single morpheme. A word like English _cats_ is two, _cat_ and _-s_.

== Documents

The **Documents** tab lists a project's texts with their word counts, searchable and paged once there are many.
**New Document** creates an empty one and opens it on its Baseline tab; click any document to open it.
A document is renamed and deleted from its own Metadata tab.

== Metadata

The **Metadata** tab holds document-level fields such as the title, speaker, date, and notes.
Which fields appear is configured per project, so a project records exactly the information its corpus needs, and a field can be held to a tagset (see <<_tagsets>>).

== Baseline

The **Baseline** tab holds the raw text you are analyzing, in whatever orthography you work in.
Paste or type it and save, one sentence per line: each line becomes a sentence, and the Tokenize tab lets you move sentence breaks afterwards.
Editing the baseline later reconciles the change with any segmentation you have already done, keeping annotations where it can.
A project can also define additional **orthographies**, alternate representations of each word such as a phonetic transcription, which appear alongside the baseline form.

== Media

The **Media** tab attaches an audio or video file to the document and holds its transcript as time-aligned segments.
Each segment is a stretch of the recording with its text, which becomes part of the baseline, and an optional speaker label.
Media is optional, and a document with none is simply a text.

To transcribe by ear, play the recording, then type the utterance you just heard into the **New segment** row and press Enter.
The segment runs from the end of the previous one to wherever playback is when you save.
Every segment is a row in the transcript, and the recording follows you: moving into a row plays that segment, Enter saves the row and moves to the next, Escape puts a row back the way it was, and Shift+Space pauses and resumes without leaving the row, starting the segment over only when playback is somewhere else.
Turn off "Play segment on entry" to play by hand.
A segment's start and end times are boxes of digits in its row: type into the box under the caret, Left and Right move between boxes, Up and Down step a box by one unit (milliseconds by 10, 100 with Shift), Enter or leaving the time saves, and a segment can never run into its neighbours.
The speed slider runs from 0.25× to 5×, and the loop button repeats the segment you are on until you pause.
Shift+Left and Shift+Right move playback one second, in a transcript row or out of one.

The timeline shows the same segments against the waveform.
Drag across an empty stretch to add a segment anywhere in the recording, drag a segment's edge to trim it, and click one to hear it and edit its row.
Times are shown to the millisecond everywhere.
Speakers are labeled per segment, and the timeline colors each speaker's segments alike.
Two segments may overlap in time only when they carry different speaker labels, which is how cross-talk is recorded; a dragged edge stops at any segment it may not overlap.
With a transcription service connected, a model can take the first pass, and its segments are marked as machine-made until you edit or confirm them (see <<_services>>).

== Tokenize

The **Tokenize** tab segments the baseline into sentences and words.
You can segment by hand, clicking between characters to split and dragging across words to merge, or run a tokenizer, either the built-in rule-based splitter or a tokenization service (see <<_services>>).
Sentences are adjusted by hand too: Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on a Mac) a word to start a new sentence at it, and the ↑ button on a sentence merges it with the previous one.
A project can mark certain tokens as **ignored**, usually punctuation, so they carry no word-level annotation and do not host morphemes.

== Analyze

The **Analyze** tab is the workspace where interlinearization happens.
It shows each sentence with its words, the morphemes under each word, and a row for every annotation field.
What you can do here:

* **Segment morphemes**: break each word into morphemes and edit their forms. A morpheme with a grammatical job and no sound is written ∅, which **Alt+0** types (see <<_typing_special_characters>>).
* **Gloss and annotate**: fill the gloss and any other word-scope or morpheme-scope fields your project defines. The editor offers faint guesses, for example a gloss it has seen before for the same form, that you can accept or override.
* **Pick from what the project already uses**: on a cell, **Alt+↓** lists every value the project has given that form, with counts. When a field is held to a tagset, the list opens on its own as you enter the cell and narrows as you type, one part at a time for a composite gloss like `1SG.NOM`.
* **Link vocabulary**: connect a word or morpheme to a shared lexicon entry for consistency, by hand or in bulk (see <<_vocabularies>>).
* **Auto-analyze**: run up to four steps over the whole document in one go: have a service translate each sentence, copy previous analyses of the same words from elsewhere in the project, have a connected service propose a segmentation and glosses for the rest, and link words and morphemes to the lexicon (see <<_services>>). Everything it produces shows in violet until you confirm it, and words you analyzed yourself are never touched.
* **Review machine work**: **Ctrl/Cmd+Enter** accepts everything proposed on a word and moves to the next, **Ctrl/Cmd+Backspace** discards a word's or a translation's unverified proposal, and **Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+↑/↓** jumps between the words and translations that still have one.
* **Fill sentence fields**: record the free translation, the speaker, and other sentence-scope values on each sentence's row.
* **Show only the rows you need**: click a row's label to choose which rows show. A minimized row stays as a thin stripe you can click back open.
* **Copy as IGT**: copy a formatted version of a sentence to the clipboard for pasting into a paper or a message, as aligned plain text, tab-separated text, LaTeX (gb4e or ExPex), or leipzig.js HTML. The LaTeX formats carry characters like ə and ∅ as they are, so typeset them with XeLaTeX or LuaLaTeX and a font that has the glyphs. They keep the characters searchable and copyable in the finished PDF, which a `tipa` transcription does not.

Plaid IGT also tracks where each value came from.
A machine-made value, such as a gloss a service proposed or an auto-made vocabulary link, shows in **violet italic** to mark it unverified: it is in the document, and waits for a person to confirm or discard it.
A guess is different: a faint gray placeholder in an empty cell that is not in the document at all until you take it with Enter.
Anything you type shows plain, and editing a machine value confirms it.
Hover over an annotation to reveal text indicating its provenance.
A value that a field's tagset refuses is underlined in red, and a closed tagset will not let it be entered at all (see <<_tagsets>>).
The exact editing gestures are collected under <<_keyboard_shortcuts>>.

== Comments

Any sentence, word, morpheme, or annotation value can carry comments: a conversation in the margin of the text, for a question to the speaker, a doubt about a gloss, or a note to a classmate.
In the Analyze tab, hover a sentence's label, a word, or a morpheme and click its comment badge to read the thread or add to it; a badge with a number marks one that already has comments.
The **Comments** tab lists every thread in the document, each with a link that jumps to its sentence.
Comments take Markdown. You can edit your own, and a maintainer can remove any.
Comments are notes about the text rather than part of it: they are not versioned with the document and do not appear in exports, except in the native archive, which carries them along.

= Vocabularies

A **vocabulary** is a shared lexicon, a list of entries (words or morphemes) that carry their own fields, such as a gloss or a part of speech.
A vocabulary lives outside any single project.
A project links to one or more of them, and while you analyze you connect a word or morpheme to an entry, by hand with **+ link** or in bulk with **Auto-analyze**.
In the link popover, the linked entry's form is a link: click it to open the entry in its vocabulary.
An entry can carry a morpheme **type** (stem, suffix, enclitic, and so on, the FieldWorks inventory). A linked morpheme takes its type from the entry, which decides how it attaches to its neighbors (`=` for clitics, `-` otherwise) in the grid and in exports; an unlinked morpheme keeps a type of its own. Only a vocabulary's maintainers can change an entry's type.

Linking keeps the same item analyzed the same way everywhere it appears, and a linked entry offers its gloss (and any other field that shares a name with one of your annotation fields) as a guess in the grid, which you accept with Enter.
Because a vocabulary is shared, editing an entry updates every document linked to it.
Vocabularies have their own area in the app, where you browse and edit entries, define the fields an entry carries, and manage who maintains them.
Which vocabularies a project draws on is part of the project's settings.

== Managing entries

The **Vocabularies** area lists every vocabulary you can see.
A vocabulary's entries are a table you can search and sort, with a **Uses** column counting the words and morphemes linked to each entry, and a **Concordance** on any entry showing every place it is linked.
Two entries may share a form, for homonyms; the table tells them apart with subscripts, in the order they were created.
**Bulk Add** takes rows pasted from a spreadsheet or read from a file, lets you map its columns onto the entry fields, and reviews each row against the entries that already exist before anything is created, so a homonym stays separate and an existing entry is never overwritten unless you say so.
The table can be downloaded as CSV.
A vocabulary's maintainers edit its entries and fields; a project's members link to them.

== Multi-word expressions

Some lexical units are longer than a word: an idiom, a phrasal verb, a compound name.
Link one to a single entry by gathering its words: **Shift+click** each word, or from one of a word's cells press **Shift+→** (and **Shift+←**) to take in its neighbours, with **Ctrl** held to skip a word that does not belong.
A dashed line shows the words gathered so far. Press **Enter** to link them to an entry, or **Esc** to give up.
The popover works as it does for one word: pick an entry, or create one. A new entry gets the words' surface forms joined by spaces, which you can edit to the citation form before creating, and the type multi-word expression, which is what FieldWorks calls a phrase, and exports as one.

A linked multi-word expression draws as a line under its words, inside the gray word band, labelled with the entry's form.
The line runs on across a line break, and a skipped word (or punctuation in between) shows as a dotted stretch.
A word can belong to several multi-word expressions at once; overlapping ones stack, and the band grows a line for each.
Each word keeps its own entry link and its own morphemes regardless.
Click a line's label to change the entry, unlink it, or confirm one that **Auto-analyze** made (it links a run of words when their joined form matches a multi-word expression entry, or one this document already has).
While that popover is open, Shift+click a word to add it to the multi-word expression or take it out.
The popover for a single word lists the multi-word expressions it is part of, and offers **Part of a multi-word expression…** to start gathering from there.

== Entry Fields

Every entry has a **Form**, its reference form (the headword), which is required.
Beyond the form, an entry carries a set of fields, and a new vocabulary starts with a few built in:

* **Morph Type**: whether the entry is a stem, a prefix, a suffix, and so on. The editor uses it to render affixes, such as the joining hyphens on the form and gloss lines. Required.
* **Gloss**: the entry's gloss, offered as a guess for the Gloss field of anything linked to it. Required.
* **POS**: the entry's part of speech.
* **Definition**: a fuller definition or note.

You can add your own fields, reorder them, and remove any except Form, Morph Type, and Gloss.
Each field can be marked **inline**, which shows it both as a column in the vocabulary table and on each entry's row in the link popover.
Other fields stay available on the entry's own edit form.

= Project Settings

A maintainer opens a project's **Settings** tab. It has five sections.

== General

The project's name, and the language it documents: its name, its ISO 639-3 code, and its Glottocode.
The language identity travels with the project into exports such as CLDF; a code that looks wrong is pointed out but still saved, since a project may document something the catalogues have no entry for.

== Text and Vocab

The extra **orthographies** each word can carry beside the baseline form, such as a phonetic transcription or a practical spelling, and which **vocabularies** the project draws on.
Deleting an orthography first tells you how many words carry it.

== Annotation

The annotation **fields** at each scope, sentence, word, and morpheme, which you can add, rename, reorder, and remove; the **ignored tokens** rule, usually punctuation, so those tokens carry no word-level annotation and host no morphemes; the project's **tagsets**; and the **document metadata** fields shown on every document's Metadata tab.
Removing a field first tells you how many values it holds.

== Tagsets

A **tagset** is a controlled list of values for a field: the parts of speech your project uses, the Leipzig abbreviations you gloss with, the genres a document can be.
Define tagsets under Annotation, then point any field at one. Several fields can share a tagset, so Gloss at the word scope and Gloss at the morpheme scope agree.

Each tagset has a mode:

* **Open**: the whole list is offered while you annotate, but anything can be typed.
* **Closed**: only values on the list can be entered. For a fixed inventory such as part of speech.
* **Mixed**: a value is allowed if it has a lowercase letter or is in a script without capitals; anything else must be on the list. Made for glosses, where a stem's gloss is a word like `dog` and a grammatical gloss like `1SG.NOM` is built from tags.

A gloss is composite, so a tagset lists the parts and names the **delimiters** that join them (for Leipzig, `.`, `:`, and `>`).
In a governed cell the list opens on its own, offers the part under the caret, and a value the tagset refuses is underlined in red.
A value can carry a description, shown beside it in the list.
**Seed from the project** fills a new tagset with the values a field already uses, so an existing corpus comes under a tagset without retyping anything.

A tagset is enforced as you type and in Bulk Edit.
An import, a service, the Assistant, or a script writes past it, so the project's **Validation** tab (maintainers) lists, for each governed field, every value in the project that its tagset refuses, with a count, and offers the two remedies: add the value to the tagset, or open the documents that use it and fix them.

== Access

The project's members and their roles, invitation links, and API access. See <<_sharing_and_permissions>>.

== Service defaults

Which service, or built-in, each task uses by default: tokenization, transcription, translation, analysis, and auto-linking (see <<_services>>).
Also what Auto-analyze's copy step copies from a word's previous analyses: its segmentation, its links, its field values, or any combination.

= Bulk Edit

The **Bulk Edit** tab (maintainers) changes many places at once, the way FieldWorks' Change Spelling does: describe the change, preview every place it would touch, tick the ones you mean, and apply them as one action in the history.
Four operations:

* **Respell words**: change the spelling of a word everywhere it occurs, keeping its morphemes, glosses, links, and other annotations. Optionally respell the same string inside morpheme forms and lexicon entry forms too.
* **Replace in a field**: find and replace across one field's values, as plain text or a regular expression, or across morpheme forms.
* **Re-analyze a word**: give every occurrence of a word the same analysis, chosen from the analyses it already has in the project.
* **Merge lexicon entries**: fold duplicate entries into one, moving every link to the survivor.

Bulk Edit respects tagsets: a replacement a closed tagset refuses is skipped, and the preview says so.

= Assistant

The **Assistant** tab is a chat with a language model that knows your project.
Ask it what is still unglossed, whether a suffix is glossed consistently, or to summarize the morphology it can see.
It reads the documents, the lexicon, the search results, and the history to answer, and shows what it read.

When you ask it to change something, it does not touch the data. It proposes a **plan**, listed step by step, which you **Approve** or **Discard**.
Approved changes are written under your own account and marked as machine-made, in violet, until you confirm them like any other proposal.
Reading needs Reader access; applying a plan needs Writer.
Conversations are private to you, kept per project, and can be copied out as Markdown.

The assistant is a service your server's operator runs and picks the model for (`plaid-igt-agent`, installed from PyPI); the tab says so when none is connected.

= Services

Plaid IGT can call external **services** for certain tasks.
A service is a small program, often a Python script, that registers itself with your project and advertises which task it handles.
Maintainers connect services under the project's **Settings**, and some tasks ship with a built-in option that needs no service at all.
There are five integration points:

* **Tokenization**: splits the baseline into words, on the **Tokenize** tab. A built-in rule-based splitter is always available and leaves sentence breaks as they are; a service can replace it with smarter segmentation of sentences and words (the bundled example uses Punkt).
* **Transcription (ASR)**: transcribes and time-aligns audio, on the **Media** tab. There is no built-in, so this needs a connected service (the bundled example uses Whisper).
* **Translation**: proposes a free translation for each sentence, as step 1 of the **Auto-analyze** dialog on the Analyze tab, so the analyzers that follow can read it. The bundled example uses a language model.
* **Analysis**: proposes a morpheme segmentation and glosses for every word, as step 3 of the same dialog. There is no built-in, so this needs a connected service (the bundled examples use PolyGloss, a multilingual glossing model, and a language model given the project's own analyses as examples). Words a person has analyzed are left alone.
* **Auto-link vocabulary**: proposes vocabulary links for unlinked words and morphemes, as step 4 of the same dialog. A built-in rule follows your project's own precedent and unique matches, and a service can propose links its own way.

The Assistant is a service too (see <<_assistant>>).
Whatever a service produces is stamped unverified, the same violet-italic state as any other machine value, so you stay in control of what gets confirmed.

= Import and Export

== Importing

Start a project from existing data on the **New Project** screen.
Currently supported formats include:

* **FieldWorks (FLEx)**: a project backup (`.fwbackup`), bringing in its texts, words, morphemes, glosses, translations, and the full lexicon. Some FLEx detail that Plaid does not model is dropped on import.
* **CLDF** (`.zip`): a Cross-Linguistic Data Formats dataset. Its examples become interlinear texts, its lexicon a vocabulary, and its language the project's identity.
* **ELAN** (`.eaf`): a set of annotation files that share one tier structure. Each file becomes a document, with its tiers, speakers, and time alignment.
* **Native Plaid IGT archive** (`.zip`): restores a project exactly, including its vocabularies, media, and comments.

== Exporting

The **Export** tab, on a document and on the project, opens a wizard where you choose a preset and a scope (a single document, several documents, or the whole project), then save the result.
Currently supported formats include:

* **Plain text**: an aligned, human-readable interlinear rendering.
* **FieldWorks (FLEx)**: the texts as FieldWorks' interlinear XML (`.flextext`) together with the project's vocabularies as a LIFT lexicon (`.lift`), zipped with a note on the order to import them in. Leave the lexicon out to get the `.flextext` on its own.
* **CLDF TextCorpus** (`.zip`): a Cross-Linguistic Data Formats dataset, one CSV per component table with a `cldf-metadata.json` describing them.
* **ELAN** (`.eaf`): one annotation file per document, bundled into a `.zip` with its recording when it has one.
* **Native Plaid IGT archive** (`.zip`): a lossless export you can import again later.

Maintainers can save reusable export presets on the project.
To grab a single sentence instead, use **Copy as IGT** in the Analyze tab.

= Search

A project's **Search** tab looks across every document.
Pick where to look: word forms, morpheme forms, any annotation field at any scope, or the lexicon's entries.
Type what to find, and choose whether a match must contain it, equal it exactly, or fit a regular expression.

**Hits** lists every match grouped by document, each in its sentence with the translation; click one to open that sentence in the Analyze tab.
**Frequencies** counts the distinct matching values instead, which is how you see every gloss a suffix has been given, or every spelling a word has had.

= History

In Plaid IGT, all changes are recorded with a meaningful message and the identity of the user that performed the change.
Additionally, past states of any document are viewable on a read-only basis.
Open a document's **history** from the rail at the left edge and pick an earlier point to view the document as it was then.
Each entry is one action as you performed it (for example "Merge morphemes" or "Tokenize"), even when it was made up of several underlying changes.
Selecting an entry shows the document as it was right after that action. Entries made of several changes have an arrow you can expand to see, and select, each individual change.
Actions performed by a service (transcription, tokenization) show up the same way, under the service's name, or under your own action when you triggered it.

= Sharing and Permissions

Projects are shared by adding members, each with a role:

* **Reader**: can view everything but make no edits.
* **Writer**: can edit documents and link vocabulary.
* **Maintainer**: can also change project settings, manage members, and delete the project.

Add and manage members under the project's **Settings**, in **Access**; members are found by their email address.
When you only have Reader access, the editor shows everything but its controls are inert.

**Invitation links** let people join without an administrator creating each account.
A maintainer mints a link that grants a role on the project, good for a set number of uses and a set number of days; whoever opens it chooses their own password and lands in the project.
A link can be revoked at any time, and it stops working on its own if the person who made it loses the authority it grants.
An administrator can also mint a password-reset link for someone who is locked out.

Scripts and services should not borrow your login.
Mint a named **API token** on your profile page instead: it can be revoked on its own, and its name appears in the history, so machine-made changes are told apart from yours.

= Typing Special Characters

Most language data needs characters a keyboard does not have.
Type a backslash followed by a two-letter code in any field that holds language data, and the code turns into the character as you finish it: `\sw` gives ə, `\ng` gives ŋ, `\?g` gives ʔ, `\ae` gives æ, `\:f` gives the length mark ː, and `\'1` gives the stress mark ˈ.

The codes are Praat's, so if you already use Praat they are the ones you know, and the full list is under Settings → Text and Vocab → Special characters.
For anything not in the list, `\u0250` and the like give any character by its Unicode number.
Type two backslashes for a plain backslash.

Nothing happens until you type the backslash, so ordinary words are never changed: `blue` stays `blue`.
This works in the Analyze grid, the baseline, the transcript, lexicon entries, and document metadata.
It does not apply where a backslash has to mean itself, such as a regular expression in Search or Bulk Edit.

**Settings → Text and Vocab → Special characters** lists every code, and each one is yours to change.
Search for a code to see what it types, point it at a different character, take it out of the project, or add a code of your own for a character none of the built-in ones cover.
A code is always two characters, and whatever you set applies to everyone working on the project.
A code you change is marked as such and can be put back with the reset button beside it, so nothing is lost by experimenting.

The empty set ∅, for a morpheme with a grammatical job and no sound, has a code of its own (`\00`) and a shortcut in the Analyze grid: **Alt+0** in a morpheme form types it.
Write it as ∅ rather than a capital O with a stroke or a zero, so it exports as a zero morph and the Validation tab does not flag it.

TIP: If you type a lot of one language, a system keyboard such as SIL Keyman is worth setting up, and it works everywhere on your computer rather than only here.

= Keyboard Shortcuts

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Shortcut | Action

| **Tab** / **Shift+Tab** | Move to the next or previous cell in the Analyze grid.
| **Enter** / **Tab** | Accept the faint guess shown in an empty cell.
| **Alt+↓** | List every value the project has given this form, with counts, and pick one.
| **-** | In a morpheme form, split the morpheme at the cursor.
| **=** | In a morpheme form, split at the cursor as a clitic boundary. The outer piece is typed as a proclitic or enclitic, so the boundary renders as `=`.
| **Backspace** | At the start of a morpheme form, merge it into the previous one.
| **Alt** + **-** / **Alt** + **=** | Enter a literal hyphen or equals sign in a morpheme form.
| **Alt+0** | In a morpheme form, type the zero morph ∅.
| **\** + a two-letter code | Type a character the keyboard lacks, anywhere language data is typed: `\sw` gives ə. See <<_typing_special_characters>>.
| **Ctrl/Cmd+Enter** | Confirm the current word's whole analysis and jump to the next.
| **Ctrl/Cmd+Backspace** | Discard the current word's unverified proposal (its machine-made morphemes, glosses, and links) and jump to the next. Anything a person made or confirmed stays.
| **Ctrl/Cmd+Shift** + arrow | Jump to the next or previous word that still has unverified proposals.
| **Ctrl/Cmd** + arrow | Move between auto-made vocabulary-link suggestions.
| **Shift+click** a word | Gather it into a multi-word expression (again to take it out). **Shift+←** / **Shift+→** from a word's cells do the same along the sentence; add **Ctrl** to skip a word.
| **Enter** (while gathering) | Link the gathered words to one entry. **Escape** drops them.
| **Escape** | Close an open popover or menu.
| **Space** (Media tab) | Play or pause the recording, when you are not typing in a box.
| **Shift+Space** (Media tab) | Play or pause the segment you are in, or the selected stretch of the timeline.
| **Shift+Left** / **Shift+Right** (Media tab) | Move playback one second back or forward.
| **Enter** / **Escape** (transcript row) | Save the row and move to the next, or put the row back the way it was.
| **Up** / **Down** (segment time) | Step the digit box under the caret: milliseconds by 10, 100 with Shift; seconds and minutes by 1, 10 with Shift. **Left** / **Right** move between boxes.

= Future Work

Plaid IGT is an alpha-quality app, with ongoing development.
Here we note some future planned work which we expect to deliver at some point.

== Richer linguistic representation

Today each annotation field holds a single plain-text value in a left-to-right script, and the lexicon is a flat list of entries.
This suits many but not all languages.
We are exploring support for multiple analysis languages and writing systems per field, including right-to-left and complex-script shaping, and per-field fonts.
A more structured vocabulary model (allowing for first-class representation of sense structures and allomorphy) may arrive at some point in the future.

== Audio and time alignment

Media support is deliberately lightweight: one recording per document and one tier of utterance-level segments.
Plaid IGT is a glossing tool, not a phonetics workbench, so word-level and finer alignment are out of scope.
For phonetic analysis, export the document to ELAN (`.eaf`) and continue there.
